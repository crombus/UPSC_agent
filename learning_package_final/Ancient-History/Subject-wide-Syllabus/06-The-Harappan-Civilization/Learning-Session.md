### ORIGIN AND TIMELINE RAIL

```text
7th millennium BCE onward: north-western farming antecedents; not a direct one-line city sequence
c. 3300-2600 BCE: Early Harappan regionalisation and proto-urban growth
c. 2600-1900 BCE: Mature Harappan urban integration
c. 1900-1300 BCE: Late Harappan localisation/deurbanisation, varying regionally
```

**Chronology caution:** use these as broad archaeological ranges. Site phases and calibrated dates may begin or end differently.

### PROGRESSIVE ROADMAP

1. Establish the evidence grammar and phased chronology.
2. Map the ecological and settlement system.
3. Lock major sites to precise features and cautions.
4. Reconstruct subsistence, craft, measurement and trade.
5. Separate secure evidence from polity, society and religion hypotheses.
6. Explain decline as regional transformation and use science within sample limits.

### SESSION 1 — TERMINOLOGY, ORIGINS AND THE EARLY-MATURE-LATE CHRONOLOGY

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

The Harappan or Indus civilisation was a Bronze Age archaeological civilisation whose wide distribution and undeciphered script require phase-based, regionally qualified reconstruction.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Use regionalisation -> integration -> localisation, not a sudden rise-and-fall story.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Harappan**
- **Indus civilisation**
- **Early-Mature-Late**
- **regionalisation**
- **integration**
- **localisation**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
REGIONAL FOOD-PRODUCING CULTURES
        |
Early Harappan: c. 3300-2600 BCE
        |
Mature urban integration: c. 2600-1900 BCE
        |
Late localisation/deurbanisation: c. 1900-1300 BCE, varying regionally
```

#### CORE TEACHING

"Harappan" is usually the safer archaeological term because settlements extended far beyond the Indus valley. The roots lay in earlier north-western farming and regional cultures, but no single village or migration explains urbanisation. Dates are broad working ranges: beginnings and endings vary by site, calibration and scholarly convention.

| Phase | Broad dates | Historical process | Diagnostic tendency |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Early Harappan | c. 3300–2600 BCE | Regionalisation and proto-urban growth | Fortified/planned settlements, regional pottery, craft and exchange |
| Mature Harappan | c. 2600–1900 BCE | Urban integration | Cities, common sign system, seals, standard weights, brick norms and wide exchange |
| Late Harappan | c. 1900–1300 BCE, varying regionally | Localisation and deurbanisation | Reduced urban traits, settlement shifts, regional pottery and diversified agriculture |

```text
regional food-producing cultures
        ↓ interaction, craft and settlement growth
Early Harappan regionalisation
        ↓ intensified coordination
Mature Harappan integration
        ↓ regionally varied transformation
Late Harappan localisation
```

⚠️ These are archaeological process phases, not three sealed peoples. Beginnings and endings vary by site and region.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Early fortified and planned settlements such as Kot Diji, Rehman Dheri and Kalibangan I show that craft, exchange and settlement hierarchy preceded Mature integration.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

Harappan urbanism developed from regional antecedents rather than appearing fully formed. Early fortified settlements such as Kot Diji and Kalibangan I already display planning, craft and exchange before the Mature phase. Their convergence helps explain the later spread of seals, weights, writing and brick norms. Yet the phase boundaries remain archaeological tools, not dates when one population suddenly replaced another.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Do not equate Early, Mature and Late phases with three separate peoples or fixed subcontinental dates.
- **Correct control:** Use regionalisation -> integration -> localisation, not a sudden rise-and-fall story.

#### MAINS USE

Open an origin or decline answer with the process sequence and qualify dates by site and region.

#### MINI RECAP

Phase labels describe changing systems; they do not name races.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

```text

  -> KEYWORDS: Harappan | Indus civilisation | Early-Mature-Late | regionalisation | integration | localisation
  -> ANSWER LINE: Use regionalisation -> integration -> localisation, not a sudden rise-and-fall story.
  -> RECAP: Phase labels describe changing systems; they do not name races.
```

### SESSION 2 — DISCOVERY, EXCAVATION AND HISTORIOGRAPHY

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Harappan historiography is the changing interpretation of an archaeological record whose contexts and methods have expanded since the first excavations.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The discovery history moved from spectacular monuments and invasion stories toward regional, household and scientific archaeology.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Cunningham**
- **Daya Ram Sahni**
- **R. D. Banerji**
- **John Marshall**
- **Wheeler**
- **context**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
1853 seal noticed -> 1921 Harappa excavation -> 1920s Mohenjo-daro/announcement
       -> city-centred interpretations -> household, landscape and scientific archaeology
```

#### CORE TEACHING

| Date/phase | Development | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| 1853 | Alexander Cunningham noticed a Harappan seal | Its civilizational significance was not recognised |
| 1921 | Daya Ram Sahni began excavation at Harappa | “Harappan” derives from the first excavated type-site |
| 1920s | R.D. Banerji worked at Mohenjo-daro; John Marshall announced the civilisation | Early interpretations reflected limited excavation and comparison |
| Later research | Expanded to villages, households, crafts, plants, animals, human remains and landscapes | Functional labels and social models remain revisable |

Because the script remains undeciphered, Harappan history is reconstructed primarily from archaeology. Material evidence is strongest for construction, production, diet, exchange and settlement; it is weaker for named rulers, language, doctrine, law and personal belief.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Daya Ram Sahni's 1921 Harappa excavation and R. D. Banerji's Mohenjo-daro work enabled John Marshall to announce the civilisation; later research widened the evidentiary field.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

The Harappan civilisation was recognised through cumulative archaeological work, not through a recovered native chronicle. Sahni at Harappa and Banerji at Mohenjo-daro supplied the contexts that Marshall publicised, while later surveys and laboratory methods shifted attention to villages, food, craft and environment. This enlarged the questions historians can ask. It also exposes the limits of early functional labels created from incomplete excavation records.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** An excavator's first label is a hypothesis, not proof of a building's function or a deity's identity.
- **Correct control:** The discovery history moved from spectacular monuments and invasion stories toward regional, household and scientific archaeology.

#### MAINS USE

Use historiography to show how interpretations of polity, religion and decline changed with evidence.

#### MINI RECAP

Better methods refine inference; they do not magically decipher the civilisation.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

```text
DISCOVERY, EXCAVATION AND HISTORIOGRAPHY
  -> KEYWORDS: Cunningham | Daya Ram Sahni | R. D. Banerji | John Marshall | Wheeler | context
  -> ANSWER LINE: The discovery history moved from spectacular monuments and invasion stories toward regional, household and scientific archaeology.
  -> RECAP: Better methods refine inference; they do not magically decipher the civilisation.
```

### SESSION 3 — GEOGRAPHICAL SPREAD, SITE MAPPING AND THE GHAGGAR-HAKRA QUESTION

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Harappan space was a vast multi-ecological network across river plains, highland routes, semi-arid zones and coasts.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Map the civilisation by ecological domains and phase, not by one rigid boundary or one river name.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Sutkagen-dor**
- **Manda**
- **Alamgirpur**
- **Malvan**
- **Shortugai**
- **Ghaggar-Hakra**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
                  MANDA
                    |
SHORTUGAI -- HARAPPA -- RAKHIGARHI -- ALAMGIRPUR
     |          |          |              |
SUTKAGEN-DOR -- MOHENJO-DARO -- KALIBANGAN
                    |
          DHOLAVIRA -- LOTHAL -- MALVAN
```

#### CORE TEACHING

“Harappan” is safer than “Indus Valley” because the distribution extended far beyond the Indus valley.

| Direction/domain | Representative evidence |
|---|---|
| West | Sutkagen-dor on the Makran coast |
| North-west outpost | Shortugai in Afghanistan near highland mineral routes |
| North | Manda in the Jammu region |
| East | Alamgirpur in western Uttar Pradesh |
| South | Malvan in southern Gujarat; Daimabad preserves southern Late-Harappan/contact evidence but should not be used mechanically as the Mature-phase extreme |
| Indus core | Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and many Sindh/Punjab settlements |
| Ghaggar-Hakra/Haryana-Rajasthan | Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi and dense settlement zones |
| Kutch/Saurashtra/Gujarat | Dholavira, Lothal, Surkotada, Desalpur, Rangpur and coastal/resource sites |

⚠️ Extent estimates vary with survey intensity, site definition and chronological phase. Use “vast multi-ecological distribution” rather than one immutable area number.

⚠️ The Ghaggar-Hakra palaeochannel and the textual Sarasvati are related but distinct research questions; physical course, site chronology and textual identification require separate evidence.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

The conventional extremes and the Afghanistan outpost demonstrate a distribution crossing the Indus, Ghaggar-Hakra, Gujarat coast and highland procurement routes.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

The term Indus Valley can mislead when treated literally. Sites from Sutkagen-dor to Alamgirpur and from Manda to southern Gujarat show a civilisation spanning several drainage and ecological zones, while Shortugai marks a distant route node. This spread explains regional differences in crops, water and architecture. It does not justify fixing every mapped site in the same phase or equating the Ghaggar-Hakra palaeochannel automatically with the Vedic Sarasvati.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Keep palaeochannel reconstruction, settlement chronology and textual Sarasvati identification as separate propositions.
- **Correct control:** Map the civilisation by ecological domains and phase, not by one rigid boundary or one river name.

#### MAINS USE

Use a west-north-east-south map sentence, then explain ecological adaptation and survey limits.

#### MINI RECAP

Wide distribution, phased occupation and contested river nomenclature must be stated together.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

```text
GEOGRAPHICAL SPREAD, SITE MAPPING AND THE GHAGGAR-HAKRA QUESTION
  -> KEYWORDS: Sutkagen-dor | Manda | Alamgirpur | Malvan | Shortugai | Ghaggar-Hakra
  -> ANSWER LINE: Map the civilisation by ecological domains and phase, not by one rigid boundary or one river name.
  -> RECAP: Wide distribution, phased occupation and contested river nomenclature must be stated together.
```

### SESSION 4 — SETTLEMENT HIERARCHY, LABOUR AND THE URBAN-RURAL SYSTEM

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Harappan urbanism was a hierarchy of cities, towns, specialist centres, villages, pastoral spaces and resource nodes.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Cities were coordinating nodes in a rural and resource-dependent system, not isolated monuments.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **settlement hierarchy**
- **urban-rural relation**
- **specialist centre**
- **surplus**
- **labour**
- **resource node**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
FARMS / HERDS / QUARRIES / COASTS
              |
VILLAGES -> SPECIALIST CENTRES -> REGIONAL TOWNS -> LARGE CITIES
   food          craft/materials       exchange       coordination
```

#### CORE TEACHING

| Settlement role | Examples | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Large cities | Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Ganweriwala | Dense residence, administration/coordination, craft, consumption and public works |
| Regional centres | Kalibangan, Banawali and other fortified towns | Agricultural-craft exchange and regional coordination |
| Specialist centres | Chanhudaro, Nageshwar, Lothal craft zones | Beads, shell, seals, metallurgy and other production |
| Villages/farmsteads | Numerous small settlements; Allahdino as a small wealthy example | Food, livestock and local production |
| Outposts/resource nodes | Shortugai and coastal/quarry sites | Raw-material procurement and route control |

⚠️ An urban civilisation does not mean every settlement was a city. Large centres depended on rural producers, pastoralists, specialists and routes.

⚠️ Standardisation implies coordination, not automatically one centralised empire.

Labour was required for brick-making, construction, drain cleaning, reservoir maintenance, craft production, transport and food provisioning. Archaeology can identify tasks and concentrations of work more securely than the legal status of workers.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Chanhudaro's craft concentration, Shortugai's route position and large cities such as Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira and Rakhigarhi reveal functional differentiation.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

Harappan cities depended on a wider productive landscape. Chanhudaro concentrated craft, Shortugai connected mineral routes, and numerous villages supplied food and animals to larger centres. Such differentiation implies organised labour and exchange across settlement levels. It does not reveal whether workers were household producers, attached specialists, seasonal labourers or state dependants in every context.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Urban civilisation does not mean most settlements were cities or that all labour was centrally commanded.
- **Correct control:** Cities were coordinating nodes in a rural and resource-dependent system, not isolated monuments.

#### MAINS USE

Link urban form to food, materials, labour and routes before discussing authority.

#### MINI RECAP

Urban splendour rested on rural production and specialised work.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

```text
SETTLEMENT HIERARCHY, LABOUR AND THE URBAN-RURAL SYSTEM
  -> KEYWORDS: settlement hierarchy | urban-rural relation | specialist centre | surplus | labour | resource node
  -> ANSWER LINE: Cities were coordinating nodes in a rural and resource-dependent system, not isolated monuments.
  -> RECAP: Urban splendour rested on rural production and specialised work.
```

### SESSION 5 — TOWN PLANNING, HOUSE FORM AND REGIONAL VARIATION

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Harappan planning combined recurring construction norms with substantial site-specific layouts and materials.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Standardisation created a shared urban grammar, not one identical city plan.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **1:2:4 brick ratio**
- **courtyard house**
- **street alignment**
- **raised sector**
- **mud brick**
- **regional adaptation**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
SHARED GRAMMAR
brick modules + streets + courtyards + controlled access
        |                     |
Mohenjo-daro/Harappa      Dholavira/Kalibangan/Banawali
baked-brick water focus   stone/local plans and variants
```

#### CORE TEACHING

##### Common tendencies

- planned streets and lanes, though not one perfect grid everywhere;
- courtyards, bathing floors, drains and wells in many houses;
- mud brick as a basic material and baked brick concentrated in water/public structures at several sites;
- commonly used Mature-phase brick proportions near 1:2:4;
- raised/enclosed sectors, fortifications or platforms;
- differentiated house sizes and craft areas.

##### Citadel/lower-town caution

“Citadel” and “lower town” are archaeological spatial labels. A raised or fortified sector does not by itself prove a palace, priestly residence or ruling-class quarter.

| Site | Architectural signature | Interpretive caution |
|---|---|---|
| Harappa | Multiple mounds, working platforms, cemetery and 12-unit building | “Granary” remains a conventional functional label |
| Mohenjo-daro | Great Bath, wells, drains, courtyard houses and public structures | “Priest-king”, “college”, “assembly hall” and “granary” are modern labels |
| Dholavira | Stone construction, multiple enclosed sectors, reservoirs and signboard | Corrects a universal two-part city model; “stadium” is interpretive |
| Kalibangan | Fortified sectors, early ploughed field and fire installations | Drainage differed from Mohenjo-daro; fire does not prove Vedic ritual |
| Lothal | Brick basin, craft workshops, drains and measurement evidence | “Dockyard” is a leading but archaeological interpretation |
| Banawali | Curved/fortified planning, moat, houses with seals/weights and plough model | Elite activity was not confined automatically to a “citadel” |
| Chanhudaro | Unfortified specialist craft town | Urban production without a conventional citadel |

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Courtyard houses and recurring brick proportions coexist with Dholavira's stone-and-multi-sector plan, Kalibangan's different drainage and Chanhudaro's unfortified craft form.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

The strongest architectural claim is coordinated diversity. Mature brick proportions, courtyard houses and ordered streets show widely shared technical norms, while Dholavira's stone construction and multi-part plan depart from a simple citadel-lower-town template. Regional builders adapted common practices to materials, water and settlement function. Therefore planning indicates communication and institutional capacity, but neither complete uniformity nor a known imperial building code.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** A raised 'citadel' is an archaeological spatial label, not automatic proof of a royal palace.
- **Correct control:** Standardisation created a shared urban grammar, not one identical city plan.

#### MAINS USE

Structure an architecture answer as shared norms -> named regional variants -> function-label caution.

#### MINI RECAP

One civilisation, several urban solutions.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

```text
TOWN PLANNING, HOUSE FORM AND REGIONAL VARIATION
  -> KEYWORDS: 1:2:4 brick ratio | courtyard house | street alignment | raised sector | mud brick | regional adaptation
  -> ANSWER LINE: Standardisation created a shared urban grammar, not one identical city plan.
  -> RECAP: One civilisation, several urban solutions.
```

### SESSION 6 — WATER, DRAINAGE AND CIVIC MAINTENANCE

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Harappan water systems joined household facilities, street drainage and site-specific storage or flood-management works.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Water architecture proves repeated technical coordination more securely than it proves a particular government.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **bathing floor**
- **covered drain**
- **soak jar**
- **well**
- **reservoir**
- **maintenance**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
WATER SOURCE -> household well/reservoir -> bathing/use
                           |
                     house drain
                           |
                street drain/soak/inspection
                           |
                 cleaning and repair norms
```

#### CORE TEACHING

##### Secure evidence

- house bathing floors linked to street drains at several sites;
- covered drains, soak jars/pits and inspection arrangements;
- public/private/neighbourhood wells, especially at Mohenjo-daro;
- the Great Bath's baked brick, gypsum mortar and bitumen waterproofing;
- Dholavira's dams, channels, cisterns and reservoir system between seasonal streams;
- site-specific flood control, platforms and water storage.

⚠️ Drains required cleaning, repair and collective norms; architecture therefore supports organised civic maintenance. It does not reveal the constitutional form of government.

⚠️ Harappan sanitation was sophisticated but not uniform. Kalibangan, Dholavira, Lothal and Mohenjo-daro used different water/drainage solutions.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Mohenjo-daro's wells and drains, the Great Bath's waterproofing and Dholavira's dams, channels and reservoirs show several scales of water engineering.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

Harappan sanitation was an operating system rather than a decorative feature. At Mohenjo-daro, wells, bathing floors and street drains linked private use to shared infrastructure, while Dholavira channelled seasonal water into storage. These arrangements required design, cleaning and recurring labour. They support civic coordination, but archaeology does not disclose whether a monarch, council, neighbourhood group or another institution directed maintenance.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Do not universalise Mohenjo-daro's drain pattern to every site or infer a named municipal office.
- **Correct control:** Water architecture proves repeated technical coordination more securely than it proves a particular government.

#### MAINS USE

Turn drains into an argument about maintenance, labour and regional adaptation.

#### MINI RECAP

Observe the system; qualify the institution.

#### CLOSING RECALL FLOW

  -> KEYWORDS: bathing floor | covered drain | soak jar | well | reservoir | maintenance

### SESSION 7 — SITE PRECISION I: HARAPPA AND MOHENJO-DARO

The two best-known cities share Mature Harappan norms but preserve different evidence for production, burial, water and public form.

- **Cemetery R-37**
- **working platforms**

- **wells**
- **Dancing Girl**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
HARAPPA                         MOHENJO-DARO
Ravi/type-site                  Indus/Sindh
R-37 + working platforms        Great Bath + wells/drains
12-unit 'granary' caution       'Priest-King' label caution
```

#### CORE TEACHING

| Site | Secure evidence | What it supports | Required caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harappa | Ravi-region type-site; multiple mounds; Cemetery R-37; craft areas; circular working platforms; seals and weights; a 12-unit building | Long urban sequence, production, mortuary evidence and coordination | The 12-unit building is conventionally called a "granary"; its exact institutional function is not textually known |
| Mohenjo-daro | Great Bath; numerous wells; courtyard houses; bathing floors and drains; workshops; bronze "Dancing Girl"; stone male bust | Civic engineering, household water access, craft and material culture | "Priest-King", "assembly hall", "college" and some building functions are modern interpretations |

The Great Bath's finely fitted baked bricks, gypsum mortar, bitumen waterproofing, stairways and controlled outlet securely establish specialised collective construction. Ritual use is plausible, but neither a named deity nor a priestly corporation follows from the structure alone.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Cemetery R-37 and working platforms at Harappa answer mortuary and production questions; the Great Bath and wells at Mohenjo-daro answer water and collective-building questions.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

Harappa and Mohenjo-daro should not be memorised as interchangeable capitals. Harappa's Cemetery R-37, craft contexts and working platforms illuminate production and burial, whereas Mohenjo-daro's Great Bath, wells and drains foreground water-centred urbanism. Their shared seals, weights and construction traditions indicate integration. The buildings called granaries and the bust called Priest-King remain modern interpretations rather than deciphered Harappan descriptions.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Great Bath belongs to Mohenjo-daro; Cemetery R-37 and the type-site association belong to Harappa.
- **Correct control:** Pair Harappa's production-mortuary evidence with Mohenjo-daro's water-built landscape.

#### MAINS USE

Use the pair to combine common standards, functional diversity and source caution.

#### MINI RECAP

Harappa = production/burial; Mohenjo-daro = water/public architecture.

### SESSION 8 — SITE PRECISION II: DHOLAVIRA AND RAKHIGARHI

- **reservoirs**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
DHOLAVIRA: seasonal streams -> dams/channels -> reservoirs -> multi-sector stone city

```

#### CORE TEACHING

| Site | Secure evidence | What it supports | Required caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dholavira | Khadir island in Kutch; stone construction; multiple enclosed sectors; dams, channels, cisterns and reservoirs; large signboard | Regional planning, water harvesting and public display of writing | The plan is not a universal Harappan template; "stadium" and precise building functions remain interpretive |

Dholavira lay between the seasonal Manhar and Mansar streams and converted a difficult water regime into a civic design problem. Rakhigarhi expands the urban map eastward and demonstrates why the civilisation cannot be reduced to two cities on the Indus.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Dholavira's reservoirs and signboard provide built and communicative evidence; Rakhigarhi's settlement, workshops, cemetery and sampled human remains connect city archaeology to laboratory study.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

Dholavira makes ecological adaptation central to Harappan urbanism. Its stone-built sectors and reservoir network converted seasonal flows into stored water, unlike a simple copy of Mohenjo-daro. Rakhigarhi, a large Haryana complex with craft and cemetery evidence, demonstrates an inland urban focus. The sites enlarge the civilisation's geography, yet neither one genome nor changing size estimates should be converted into civilisation-wide certainty.

#### UPSC TRAP

- **Wrong shortcut:** Dholavira has the signboard and reservoirs; one Rakhigarhi individual cannot define every Harappan population.
- **Correct control:** Dholavira demonstrates adaptive water urbanism; Rakhigarhi demonstrates the scale and scientific potential of the eastern domain.

#### MAINS USE

Use Dholavira for adaptation and Rakhigarhi for scale plus scientific-inference limits.

#### MINI RECAP

Both sites correct an Indus-core-only narrative.

### SESSION 9 — SITE PRECISION III: KALIBANGAN, LOTHAL AND BANAWALI

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

These regional centres connect farming, craft, water, planning and interpretation debates.

> A site-feature answer earns marks when it adds the function caution to the correct location.

- **ploughed field**
- **fire installations**
- **brick basin**
- **dockyard caution**
- **plough model**
- **moat**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
KALIBANGAN -> ploughed field + fire installations

### SESSION 20 — ENVIRONMENT, RIVERS, PALAEOCLIMATE AND MULTI-CAUSAL DEURBANISATION

                                  |

```

#### CORE TEACHING

After c. 1900 BCE, major urban traits declined unevenly:

- large cities contracted or were abandoned;
- script, seals, long-distance standards and some crafts diminished;
- settlement distributions shifted;
- regional pottery traditions became more prominent;
- agriculture diversified;
- smaller/rural communities continued.

##### Cause families

| Cause family | Possible contribution | Why it cannot stand alone |
|---|---|---|
| River change/flood | Damaged or redirected particular settlements | Different sites had different hydrologies |
| Aridity/monsoon stress | Raised water and agricultural risk | Chronology/proxy scale and social response vary |
| Trade contraction | Reduced demand/raw-material circulation | Internal production and rural life continued |
| Social/political reorganisation | Weakened integrative institutions | Direct institutional evidence is scarce |
| Disease/resource pressure | Possible local stress | Evidence remains incomplete |
| Invasion/incursion | Contact or violence may occur locally | No evidence supports one Aryan invasion destroying the civilisation |

⚠️ Prefer **deurbanisation, localisation and transformation** to “sudden extinction”.

⚠️ Climate, invasion, flood or river shift should not be presented as one universal cause.

##### Continuities may include

- crops, livestock and rural settlement;
- some pottery and craft traditions;
- regional population continuity and interaction;
- selected symbols or practices.

##### Discontinuities include

- urban scale and integrated settlement networks;
- script use;
- seal system and standardisation;
- aspects of long-distance exchange and specialised production.

⚠️ A similar motif, crop or ritual does not prove an unchanged Harappan-to-later tradition.

⚠️ Detailed Harappan–Vedic identity, language and genetics belong to topic 07. Topic 06's safe conclusion is continuity plus major institutional discontinuity.

#### NAMED EVIDENCE / EXAMPLE

Post-1900 BCE contraction of major cities, reduced script/seal use, changing settlement geography and regionalised material traditions demonstrate transformation rather than disappearance.

#### CLAIM -> EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

No single cause fits every Harappan region. River shifts or floods affected particular settlements, climatic stress altered agricultural risk, exchange contraction reduced some urban demand, and political-economic reorganisation may have weakened integrative institutions. The archaeological result was uneven contraction, migration and localisation after about 1900 BCE. Continued rural settlements and regional traditions rule out both instant extinction and a universally proven invasion.

- It is relevant to population history but cannot represent every Harappan region, identify language, caste, religion or polity, or settle the full Aryan question.

Crops, craft skills, settlement knowledge and selected symbols may continue, while urban scale, script and integrated institutions clearly changed.

#### PYQ 5 — UPSC Mains 2025, GS-I, Q1

**Question:** Discuss the salient features of the Harappan architecture. (Answer in 150 words.)

**Demand:** "Discuss" requires defining features, named examples, regional variation and a bounded conclusion.

**Model answer:** Harappan architecture combined civic coordination with regional adaptation. Planned streets, side-lane entrances and courtyard houses created ordered residential blocks. At Mohenjo-daro, wells, bathing floors and covered drains connected households to shared sanitation; the waterproof Great Bath demonstrates specialised collective construction. Standard mud and baked bricks, commonly using a 1:2:4 proportion in the Mature phase, enabled modular building. Yet plans varied: Dholavira used stone, multiple enclosed sectors and reservoirs adapted to seasonal streams, while Kalibangan had different wastewater arrangements. Lothal's brick basin and Harappa's large storage-related buildings indicate specialised functions, but labels such as "dockyard", "granary" and "citadel" remain archaeological interpretations. Thus Harappan architecture was planned, water-conscious and technically standardised, but regionally diverse and textually anonymous.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the directive with construction, domestic form, water, public works, four named sites, variation and source criticism within the required limit.

**Question:** Explain how Dholavira modifies the conventional picture of Harappan town planning. (Answer in 150 words.)

**Model answer (102 words):**

Dholavira modifies the conventional model by showing that Harappan cities did not follow one Indus-core plan. Located on Khadir island in Kutch, it used extensive stone construction, several enclosed sectors and a large signboard rather than a simple universal citadel-lower-town division. These features demonstrate shared Harappan capacities for planning, measurement and public labour, but also strong regional adaptation to material and hydrological conditions. Dholavira therefore supports coordinated diversity: it belongs firmly to Mature Harappan urbanism while disproving architectural uniformity.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why must religious interpretations of Harappan objects remain cautious? (Answer in 150 words.)

**Model answer (108 words):**

Harappan religion is reconstructed without a deciphered text. The horned seated figure, varied female figurines, pipal and animal motifs, fire installations, burials and the Great Bath can indicate symbolic or ritual practices. The evidence supports ritual diversity, but not a securely named pantheon, uniform state religion or unbroken continuity with later Hindu practice.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse the relationship between Harappan cities, rural production, craft specialisation and labour. (Answer in 250 words.)

**Model answer (151 words):**

Harappan urbanism functioned through an interdependent settlement hierarchy. Villages, farms and pastoral zones supplied grain, fibre, livestock and labour; quarry, mining and coastal zones supplied stone, metals and shell. Cities and specialist centres converted these inputs into beads, seals, faience, textiles, pottery and copper-bronze objects. Chanhudaro demonstrates concentrated craft without a conventional citadel, while Lothal links workshops, measurement and a large basin to exchange. Shortugai shows procurement reach toward highland mineral corridors.

Standard weights, repeated seal forms and transport by carts, river craft and coastal routes made dispersed production comparable and movable. Public works at Mohenjo-daro and Dholavira also required brick-making, construction and recurring maintenance labour.

Yet archaeology cannot identify the legal status of workers or the exact balance among household production, attached specialists, market exchange and redistribution. The evidence therefore supports organised, differentiated labour within a connected urban-rural economy, not a self-sufficient city or a fully reconstructed state labour code.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Does Harappan standardisation prove a centralised empire? Discuss. (Answer in 250 words.)

**Model answer (151 words):**

Standardised weights, seals, brick proportions and aspects of urban planning show remarkable integration across the Mature Harappan world. Cubical weights enabled comparable quantities; common seal practices supported communication or transactions; modular bricks aided construction; and major water works indicate organised labour. Such regularity makes sustained coordination undeniable.

It does not, however, identify the political mechanism. Dholavira's stone-built multi-sector plan, Kalibangan's distinct drainage and Chanhudaro's specialist form show regional variation within the shared system. More importantly, the undeciphered script has yielded no secure king-list, dynasty or administrative constitution, while no unambiguous palace, royal tomb or monumental temple identifies a ruling house.

Three models therefore remain open: a centralised state, several regional states or city-states, and corporate or decentralised elites linked by shared institutions and exchange. Standardisation is evidence of capacity and norm enforcement, but not a signature unique to empire. The most defensible verdict is strong civilisational coordination with unresolved political form.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the decline of Harappan urbanism as a multi-causal regional transformation. (Answer in 250 words.)

**Model answer (187 words):**

After about 1900 BCE, the integrated urban system weakened unevenly rather than disappearing in one catastrophe. Large cities contracted or were abandoned; script, seals, long-distance standards and some specialised crafts declined; settlement patterns and pottery traditions became more regional.

Several mechanisms may have interacted. River movement and recurrent flooding affected particular settlements; weakening monsoons or aridity raised agricultural and water risk; changes in Gulf-Mesopotamian exchange reduced some external demand; and internal political-economic reorganisation may have undermined institutions that coordinated labour and circulation. None explains every region. A palaeoclimate proxy has a scale and date, and the Ghaggar-Hakra/Sarasvati identification is separate from demonstrating its effect on a settlement. The invasion model also lacks evidence for one conquering event that destroyed the civilisation.

The Late Harappan record supplies the necessary qualification. Communities continued in smaller or shifted settlements, agriculture diversified, and selected pottery, craft and subsistence practices persisted, even as cities, writing and integrated standards diminished.

Thus "decline" means regionally varied deurbanisation and localisation: major institutional discontinuity combined with demographic and cultural continuity. A good explanation connects cause, local mechanism, chronology and response instead of listing universal theories.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate what archaeology and scientific evidence can—and cannot—tell us about Harappan society and legacy. (Answer in 250 words.)

**Model answer (199 words):**

Archaeology reconstructs Harappan life most securely through material practices. Houses, drains and reservoirs reveal construction and maintenance; crop, animal and residue evidence reconstruct diet; workshops and provenance identify production and exchange; burials and skeletal study illuminate mortuary treatment, health and mobility. Radiocarbon dating refines chronology, while isotopes and ancient DNA can test bounded questions about movement and ancestry.

These methods also impose limits. Unequal houses or grave goods may indicate differentiated access, but do not directly prove caste, slavery or a complete class hierarchy. Figurines and seals may preserve ritual imagery, but cannot name deities while the script remains undeciphered. The published Rakhigarhi genome concerns one context-specific individual; it cannot identify the language, religion or total ancestry of every Harappan population. Environmental proxies likewise require regional and chronological matching.

Legacy should therefore be divided into continuity and discontinuity. Crops, craft knowledge, rural settlement and some symbols or practices may continue through regional interaction, while urban scale, script, seals and integrated standardisation clearly declined.

Scientific evidence makes Harappan history more precise by narrowing claims, not by authorising total identities. The defensible legacy is selective continuity through transformed communities, not an unchanged civilisation or a biological verdict on language and culture.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> This block is enrichment. It is not required for a competent core answer.

### ADVANCED 1 — HISTORIOGRAPHY OF STATE AND URBANISM

Older empire, priest-king and invasion models often treated monumental sectors as direct institutions. Later household, craft, landscape and political-economy approaches allow several authority models. Use the historiography to explain why the evidence can establish integration more securely than a specific constitution.

### ADVANCED 2 — HOUSEHOLD AND CRAFT ARCHAEOLOGY

Activity areas, production debris, access routes and house modification can recover work below the level of the city plan. They sharpen questions about household production and specialised labour, but a workshop does not automatically identify a guild, caste or state factory.

### ADVANCED 3 — SCRIPT-METHOD DEBATES

Sign frequency, positional pattern, direction, medium and seal context remain legitimate subjects of analysis. Estimates of the number of signs depend on how variants are counted. No proposed language or decipherment has achieved secure acceptance; avoid presenting computational pattern as translation.

### ADVANCED 4 — ARCHAEOMETRY AND PROVENANCE

Petrography, elemental analysis and experimental archaeology can distinguish material sources and production sequences. Their answer value lies in connecting a finished object to procurement and technique. A chemical match establishes material movement more directly than it establishes the identity or political status of the mover.

### ADVANCED 5 — PALAEOCLIMATE, RIVERS AND SCALE

Lake, speleothem, sediment and channel studies can indicate changing hydrology or monsoon conditions. Every proxy has spatial resolution, chronological uncertainty and a mechanism problem. It must be connected to the occupation history of the relevant site; it cannot settle the textual Sarasvati question by itself.

### ADVANCED 6 — GENETICS AND BIOARCHAEOLOGY

The 2019 Rakhigarhi ancient-DNA result is important but sample-bounded. Ancient DNA, isotopes, skeletal pathology and dental evidence can address ancestry, mobility, diet and health only within preservation and sampling limits. Language, religion, polity and the complete history of a civilisation cannot be read from one individual.

### ADVANCED 7 — EGYPT AND MESOPOTAMIA: A BOUNDED COMPARISON

Egypt and Mesopotamia offer readable royal or administrative texts and conspicuous royal/temple monuments. Harappan evidence foregrounds civic water, settlement and standardisation without securely identified kings or monumental temples. This contrast concerns surviving evidence and institutional display; it is not a ranking of complexity or proof that Harappans lacked authority.
