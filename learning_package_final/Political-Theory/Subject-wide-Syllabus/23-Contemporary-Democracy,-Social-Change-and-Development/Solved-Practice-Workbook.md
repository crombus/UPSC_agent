### Cross-owned descriptive PYQ transfer: Philosophy Paper II (verified local ledger)

**2025, 15 marks - Development and Social Progress:** Is it possible to reconcile the concept of development with tribal values to bring social and economic progress? Discuss.

**Independent model:** Reconciliation requires asking who defines progress and whose losses count. Gauba's development account goes beyond increased production to human satisfaction and social capacity; a tribal community's collective relation to land can itself be a source of welfare, not merely an obstacle to growth. For an Indian forest-dependent settlement, improved health services and access to markets may bring real benefits, but a project imposed without meaningful consultation can destroy livelihoods and ecological knowledge. Gandhi's stress on labour dignity and need-based consumption supplies a moral check on the assumption that extraction is the only path. A participatory appraisal would let affected people negotiate land use, remedies and benefits, while constitutional rights still restrain coercion. Romanticising hardship is equally mistaken: no community must forgo healthcare in the name of cultural authenticity. Development can be reconciled with tribal values if choice, material security and ecological continuity are assessed together; the answer must acknowledge that trade-offs remain. A further distinction is between protecting a cultural practice and forcing every member of a community to conform to it. Women and younger members may disagree internally; meaningful consent must hear them too. Where a project creates unavoidable costs, publicly negotiated safeguards and review of implementation are stronger than ceremonial consultation. The moral standard is neither assimilation nor isolation, but equal agency alongside secure livelihoods.

**Examiner emphasis:** Oppose forced assimilation AND romanticised deprivation; name agency, services and ecology.

**2024, 15 marks - Development and Social Progress:** Is economic development a necessary condition, sufficient condition, both or neither, in order to achieve social progress? Give reasons and justifications for your answer.

**Independent model:** Economic development expands the resources that may finance services and security, but it cannot by itself prove moral progress. Gauba distinguishes development, a growth of institutional capability, from progress, an evaluation of better lives. India's capacity to provide public education or health can improve with resources; whether disadvantaged groups actually gain access depends on allocation, accountability and the removal of social exclusion. Growth accompanied by pollution or political silencing can improve one indicator and worsen other dimensions. Conversely, reform of discriminatory rules can advance equal standing without waiting for a prior income threshold. Calling economic development strictly necessary would ignore those political and ethical gains; calling it sufficient would mistake means for outcomes. In practice a material base often assists durable progress, especially where deprivation is acute, but its moral significance depends on distribution, civil liberty and ecological limits. Thus it is neither a logically necessary nor a sufficient condition, though it is frequently an important enabling one. This distinction also exposes a political test: if richer production increases elite capacity while ordinary people lose opportunities to object, the resulting economy is stronger in one respect but society may not be more just. Publicly contestable distribution and environmental review are therefore not obstacles added after growth; they determine whether its benefits translate into better lives.

**Examiner emphasis:** Address both necessity and sufficiency separately; distinguish conceptual and practical claims.

**2023, 15 marks - Development and Social Progress:** In the present scenario, will the emphasis on skill education enhance development? Evaluate.

**Independent model:** Skill education can raise productive capacity and workers' agency, but training alone cannot ensure a job or social progress. Gauba's broad concept of development asks whether institutions become effective and satisfy human needs. For an Indian district, locally relevant vocational instruction may help workers adapt to changing work and enable small enterprises; practical learning can also dignify labour in Gandhi's sense. Yet quality, access for marginalised learners, employer demand and fair working conditions determine whether the new skills yield secure livelihoods. A course advertised without placements or credible work opportunities merely shifts responsibility to students. Moreover, judging training only by wages misses civic capacities and the ecological consequences of the work it supports. A good evaluation links education with employment policy, non-discriminatory access, public review and sustainable local needs. Skill emphasis is a potentially valuable input to development, not an automatic substitute for redistribution or accountable institutions. Compare two apprenticeships with identical curricula: one feeds a local cooperative offering stable work; the other has no employer demand and leaves graduates without bargaining power. The first improves capability through a credible work pathway, whereas the second merely certifies attendance. The state and employers must therefore share responsibility for opportunities rather than using training to blame workers for structural unemployment.

**Examiner emphasis:** Specify causal pathway and labour-demand limit; do not insert unsourced contemporary statistics.

**2022, 20 marks - Development and Social Progress:** Do you agree that economic development does not on its own lead to human development and social progress? Give reasons and justifications for your answer.

**Independent model:** I agree: output and institutional capacity are means, whereas human development asks whose capabilities expand, and progress is a further moral verdict. Gauba's distinctions prevent these concepts from being used interchangeably. A rise in India's aggregate production could help finance public services; it does not show whether a marginalised village can reach healthcare, speak at local meetings or avoid polluted drinking water. A market-led path can expand choice and output but may externalise ecological and distributive costs. Welfare policies can reduce deprivation, though fiscal weakness or poor implementation can limit their reach. Socialist planning may prioritise social rights, but suppression of opposition would undermine an equally important human capability. Gandhi challenges the underlying equation of ever-growing consumption with a good life. Political development, too, requires responsive institutions and state capacity, not merely more resources. The counterpoint is that persistent scarcity makes many social promises difficult to deliver: economic development is often enabling. It is not sufficient without inclusive distribution, civil freedom, accountability and sustainability. Measure improvement through effective agency and durable welfare, not the growth rate alone. Consider also the tension between electoral authorization and material agency. A community can have a vote yet lack transport to a clinic or control over a damaging project; treating that community as already empowered mistakes formal access for usable freedom. Participation in planning and enforceable remedies make growth answerable to those who bear its costs. But welfare spending without administrative capability may also miss intended beneficiaries. The appropriate standard joins financing, implementation, scrutiny and ecological continuity rather than simply opposing markets to public intervention.

**Examiner emphasis:** Separate economic means, human capabilities and normative progress; include enabling counterargument.

**2020, 20 marks - Development and Social Progress:** State and examine the Gandhian concept of social development.

**Independent model:** Gandhi judges development by the quality of human relationships and restraint, not the multiplication of wants. His stress on bread labour gives productive work dignity; need-based consumption opposes an order in which a minority's luxury determines everyone's economic direction. In an Indian village, locally accountable decisions about work, common resources and basic services would better fit this ethic than an imposed project that enriches outsiders while displacing residents. The approach also anticipates ecological limits: extraction can deplete the basis of future livelihoods even when today's output rises. Compared with market development, Gandhi questions whether higher production alone improves welfare; compared with socialist central planning, he distrusts overconcentration and stresses moral agency. An objection is that restraint by itself cannot fund complex health systems or provide sufficient employment to all in a large economy. The reply is not to freeze technology or romanticise poverty, but to subject production to tests of equitable livelihood, local voice and environmental durability. Gandhian social development remains a critical ethical standard, not a ready-made blueprint for every industrial institution. His village-centred orientation suggests that those who live with a development decision should have a voice in its design, which links economic ethics to political participation. Still, local power can also be unequal: class, caste and gender hierarchies can silence people within a village. Decentralisation is therefore valuable only with protected dissent and opportunities for disadvantaged residents to influence decisions. A contemporary application combines local initiative with wider public services and enforceable rights. That qualified synthesis avoids presenting small-scale production as a sufficient remedy for deprivation.

**Examiner emphasis:** Explain bread labour, self-restraint and decentralisation; test feasibility without caricature.

**2019, 15 marks - Development and Social Progress:** Does technological development lead to progress in the ethical standards of the society? Explain.

**Independent model:** Technological development expands capacity; moral progress requires a separate judgment about justice and human conduct. Gauba's distinction matters because new tools may improve access to services while also enabling exclusion or manipulation. An Indian village's digital grievance channel could help residents hold officials accountable if every group can use it and receive reasoned responses; if inaccessible to those without connectivity or filtered without oversight, the same tool may silence them. Dewey's insistence on communicative participation explains why faster transmission is not automatically better deliberation. Gandhi's caution about multiplying wants likewise asks whether technical efficiency serves human needs or concentrates power. Technology can make ethical action easier by spreading information and exposing abuse, but neither devices nor algorithms compel empathy, equal voice or lawful restraint. Institutions must secure access, privacy, public challenge and remedies. Hence technology can facilitate ethical progress when governed by accountable and inclusive norms, but the inference from technical advance to moral advance is invalid. Consider an automated allocation of public benefits: faster processing might reduce arbitrary delay, but hidden criteria could reproduce bias and deny a claimant a hearing. The ethical gain lies not in speed alone but in transparent criteria, error correction and the claimant's ability to contest outcomes. This makes moral progress a question about institutional design and equal standing, not the mere arrival of new machinery.

**Examiner emphasis:** Use the development/progress distinction, paired effects and conditional verdict.

**2026, 10 marks - Political Ideologies:** "On what grounds does Marxism criticize pure democracy ?"

**Independent model:** Marxism questions the claim that legally equal votes alone express equal social power. Ownership of production divides society into classes with unequal resources to organise, influence policy and shape public ideas. A state formally representing all citizens may therefore protect the economic position of those who control property. The distinction between the abstract equal citizen and unequal material lives explains why periodic selection of rulers may not emancipate workers. Gramsci's account of hegemony adds that consent can be formed through civil society, not simply imposed by police power. The constructive Marxist demand is for material conditions and institutions that make collective self-government real. **2026, 15 marks - Development and Social Progress:** "Examine the relationship between democracy and development. Does democracy promote or hinder development ?"

**Independent model:** The answer depends first on what development measures. If it means only speed of a single project, public objections may seem to delay it; if it means effective institutions, security of livelihood and durable human capability, accountable participation can improve its design. Gauba distinguishes higher output from welfare, political capacity and sustainability. Open criticism can disclose hidden costs, including displacement and polluted water, before they become irreversible. Participation can make policies responsive to those who bear the burdens; deliberation adds reasons that decision-makers must answer. The strongest contrary case is that elections reward immediate benefits, organised interests can block needed reform and repeated consultation can exhaust administrative capacity. These are genuine coordination problems, not proof that coercion is development. Institutions need published reasons, protected opposition and capable implementation so public scrutiny leads to correction rather than permanent stalemate. Nor does a formally democratic ballot guarantee inclusive development when economic inequality prevents citizens from using their rights. The relationship runs both ways: social security and education can make participation more effective, while accountable choice can steer investment toward publicly defensible ends. Democracy generally promotes inclusive development when combined with capacity and rule of law; it may hinder particular decisions when its procedures are captured, but removing dissent is no solution.

**Examiner emphasis:** Two-way conditional relationship, serious efficiency objection and qualified verdict; do not name a present-day country as a democratic or authoritarian development example.
