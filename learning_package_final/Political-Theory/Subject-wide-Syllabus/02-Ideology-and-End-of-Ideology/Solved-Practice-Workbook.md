### MCQ 1

Which statement captures the two principal meanings of ideology used in the chapter?

- A. A doctrine held by a party, and a science that studies how ideas form and become distorted.
- B. A constitutional programme, and an empirical survey of voter preferences.
- C. A moral theory of private conduct, and a legal code enforced by the state.
- D. A historical narrative of past regimes, together with a statistical technique for predicting election outcomes.

**Answer: A**

**Option-by-option explanation**
- **A:** A correctly joins ideology as committed idea-set with ideology as science of ideas.
- **B:** B substitutes institutional and polling categories for the chapter's two meanings.
- **C:** C shifts the concept into private morality and positive law.
- **D:** D confuses ideology with historiography and electoral forecasting.

> **Examiner trap:** Remembering only ideology-as-belief and missing the older science-of-ideas meaning.

### ORIGINAL GS MAINS PRACTICE WITH MODEL SOLUTIONS

### Original GS Mains Model 1 — 10 marks

**Question:** Explain the two meanings of ideology and distinguish ideology from political theory. Answer in 150 words.

**Demand decode:** the directive `explain` requires the answer to set out the concept, its mechanism and its consequences in a stated order. Fix the boundary of the two meanings of ideology and distinguish ideology from political theory in the opening line, carry the argument on Ideology, Gauba's, Destutt and Tracy's, and reserve the closing sentences for the qualification that the decisive test is therefore not whether values are present, but whether claims remain open to reasons, counter-evidence and revision.

**Model answer (139 words):**

Ideology has two meanings in Gauba's account. First, it is a set of ideas accepted by a group and used to justify an order, identify opponents and mobilise action. Second, in Destutt de Tracy's original sense, it is a science of ideas examining how beliefs form and become distorted. Political theory differs mainly in intellectual posture. Ideology begins from commitment and seeks adherence; theory submits claims to evidence, conceptual clarification and normative criticism. Thus, nationalism or environmentalism may organise collective action, while theory asks whether their factual claims, means and goals are defensible. The distinction is qualified: theory arises in historical situations and can become ideological when assumptions are insulated from criticism. Conversely, an ideology may contain emancipatory insight. The decisive test is therefore not whether values are present, but whether claims remain open to reasons, counter-evidence and revision.

**Why this earns marks:** it obeys `explain` instead of drifting into description, attaches each claim to named evidence (Ideology, Gauba's, Destutt and Tracy's), converts that evidence into analysis of the two meanings of ideology and distinguish ideology from political theory, and keeps the examiner-facing qualification that the decisive test is therefore not whether values are present, but whether claims remain open to reasons, counter-evidence and revision. At 139 words it stays inside the 150-word GS ceiling for 10 marks.

### Original GS Mains Model 2 — 10 marks

**Question:** How does Marx's use of ideology differ from Lenin's use of the term? Answer in 150 words.

**Demand decode:** the directive `how` requires the answer to state the mechanism step by step and show what each step produces. Fix the boundary of does Marx's use of ideology differ from Lenin's use of the term in the opening line, carry the argument on Marx, Material, Ideology and Lenin, and reserve the closing sentences for the qualification that marx and Lenin therefore share the link between ideas and class struggle, but differ in range: Marx emphasises distortion by ruling ideas, whereas Lenin also legitimises an organised revolutionary ideology.

**Model answer (140 words):**

Marx uses ideology mainly as a critical concept. Material relations shape consciousness, and ruling ideas present historically specific class arrangements as natural or universal. Ideology is therefore false consciousness that helps dominant interests reproduce power; it need not be a deliberate lie. Lenin broadens the term into a more neutral category of class consciousness. Because class struggle continues during the socialist phase, workers require proletarian ideology—scientific socialism—to resist bourgeois influence and organise transformation.
