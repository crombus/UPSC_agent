- ✅ UPSC repeatedly tests whether a student can distinguish theory from ideology, and neutral analysis from partisan justification; this chapter provides that distinction directly (Gauba, PDF pp.29-31).

### SESSION 2 — Essential Vocabulary of Ideology Critique

#### 3. Essential definitions
| Term | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideology (set of ideas) | ✅ A set of ideas accepted as true by a group, party or nation without further examination, often used to justify or denounce a social or political arrangement (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29). |
| Ideology (science of ideas) | ✅ A study of how ideas are formed, distorted and purified so that true knowledge may be separated from false knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31). |
| Obscurantism | ✅ A deliberate effort to keep things obscure so that people do not know the truth (Gauba, PDF p.29). |
| Sociology of knowledge | ✅ Inquiry into how knowledge is socially determined, conditioned or distorted; Gauba links the term especially with Mannheim (Gauba, PDF p.31). |
| Critical theory | ✅ A stream of thought that studies society from the angle of deviation from rational human emancipation, not by simply copying natural-science paradigms (Gauba, PDF p.31). |
| Utopia | ✅ A fascinating but unrealizable vision or set of principles used by Mannheim to contrast with ideology (Gauba, PDF p.34). |
| Totalitarianism | ✅ A regime-form seeking to direct all aspects of life toward state-defined goals and suppress criticism or alternative aims (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). |
| Open society | ✅ A society with free flow of information, criticism, policy revision and no monopoly claim to truth (Gauba, PDF p.35). |

### SESSION 3 — The Chapter Spine: Distortion, Mobilisation and Closure

#### 4. Core argument / chapter spine
1. ✅ Gauba first explains ideology as action-oriented group belief used to justify power, attack opponents, motivate sacrifice or spur social transformation (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29).
2. ✅ He then distinguishes political theory from ideology by insisting that theory critically evaluates claims, whereas ideology pursues partisan ends from a committed standpoint (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30).
3. ✅ Next, he returns to the older meaning of ideology as science of ideas, beginning with Destutt de Tracy and the wider scientific concern with valid versus distorted knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31).
4. ✅ Marx, Lukacs and Mannheim are then introduced as major analysts of ideology as distortion, class consciousness or social conditioning of knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.32-34).
5. ✅ Popper and Arendt redirect the issue toward totalitarianism, showing how ideology can become a closed, state-backed claim to absolute truth (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36).
6. ✅ The chapter closes with the end-of-ideology debate, where Bell, Dahrendorf, Lipset, Rostow and Galbraith present historically situated claims about affluent industrial societies and the waning of sharp ideological division (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39).
7. ⚠️ The deeper spine is this: ideology can be both a motivating framework and an object of criticism, so the task of theory is neither blind celebration nor lazy dismissal.

### SESSION 4 — Theory, Ideology, Utopia and Open Inquiry

#### 5. Essential distinctions
| Distinction | What to say precisely |
|---|---|
| Ideology vs theory | ✅ Ideology is interested, action-oriented and justificatory; theory is disinterested, critical and validity-testing (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30). |
| Set of ideas vs science of ideas | ✅ The first sense mobilizes adherents; the second sense studies how ideas arise and become distorted (Gauba, PDF pp.28, 30-31). |
| Closed mind vs open mind | ✅ Gauba's dimensions table opposes ideology-as-faith to science-of-ideas as critical examination and openness to questioning authority (Gauba, PDF p.32). |
| Ideology vs utopia | ✅ Mannheim treats both as forms of false consciousness: ideology conserves the status quo, utopia energizes change through unrealizable projection (Gauba, PDF p.34). |
| Open society vs totalitarian society | ✅ In Popper's contrast, open society allows criticism and revision; totalitarian society claims final truth and mobilizes resources through ideology (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). |
| Historical thesis vs settled fact | ⚠️ "End of ideology" is a context-bound thesis about mid-twentieth-century industrial societies, not a permanent law of politics. |

### SESSION 5 — From Tracy and Bacon to Marx and Lenin

#### 6. Thinkers and positions
| Thinker | Position in brief |
|---|---|
| Destutt de Tracy | ✅ First used ideology as the science of ideas and tied valid knowledge to empirical learning (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31). |
| Francis Bacon | ✅ Earlier warned that knowledge can be distorted by false impressions or "idols," anticipating later critique of distorted ideas (Gauba, PDF p.31). |
| Karl Marx | ✅ Ideology is false consciousness linked to material conditions and dominant-class rule (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33). |
| V.I. Lenin | ✅ Proletariat too needs ideology, namely scientific socialism, because class struggle persists in the socialist phase (Gauba, PDF p.33). |
| Karl Mannheim | ✅ All thought is socially situated; ideology conserves, utopia agitates, and sociology of knowledge seeks a more objective synthesis (Gauba, PDF pp.33-34). |
| Karl Popper | ✅ Ideology belongs to totalitarian societies claiming final truth, not to open societies that permit criticism (Gauba, PDF p.35). |
| Hannah Arendt | ✅ Totalitarianism is marked by ideology plus terror and depends on specific social and political conditions of mass uprootedness (Gauba, PDF p.35). |
| Daniel Bell / Dahrendorf / Lipset | ✅ They advance historical end-of-ideology arguments about Western/post-capitalist/post-industrial societies in the 1950s-60s (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39). |

### SESSION 6 — Lukacs, Mannheim, Popper and Arendt

#### 7. Illustrative examples
- ✅ Nationalism may mobilize sacrifice for freedom, whereas communalism may mobilize hatred and destruction; Gauba uses these contrasts to show ideology's action-orientation (Gauba, PDF p.29).
- ✅ Environmentalism is given as an example of an ideology that may challenge destructive arrangements rather than merely defend vested interests (Gauba, PDF p.29).
- ✅ Gauba's contrast between Aristotle and Machiavelli shows theory's critical role: some historically situated claims are rejected, while durable insights are retained after scrutiny (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30).
- ⚠️ A clean classroom example is this: an ideology says "our model alone is just"; political theory asks what evidence, concepts and moral grounds support or weaken that claim.

#### 12. Full reconstruction: Marx, Lukács and Mannheim on distorted/situated consciousness
- ✅ **Marx — false consciousness:** ideology is consciousness that lags behind material development and helps sustain the dominant class's interest by presenting a sectional arrangement (e.g. private ownership of the means of production) as natural or universal rather than as a historically specific, class-serving arrangement (Gauba, PDF p.32).
- ✅ **Lukács — class consciousness:** deepens the class-consciousness line by treating consciousness as always class-situated, and argues the proletariat's structural position uniquely opens a route toward a more universal consciousness — a stronger and more specific claim than Marx's general "false consciousness" (Gauba, PDF p.33).
- ⚠️ **Reification (supplementary vocabulary, no specific page anchor claimed):** commonly associated with Lukács's wider theory of class consciousness, reification names the process by which historically produced social relations between people (e.g. the wage relation) come to appear as fixed, natural relations between things, obscuring their origin in class domination and making them seem unchangeable. This term is standard in secondary political-theory literature on Lukács but is used here only as an analytical gloss, not as a claim independently verified against the specific Gauba page range used to build this file — do not attribute a specific book title, publication year or page number to it beyond that caution.
- ✅ **Mannheim — situated knowledge:** generalizes the point beyond class-enemy accusation: all thought is socially situated; ideology conserves the existing order, utopia energizes change through unrealizable projection, and the "sociology of knowledge" studies this social conditioning of ideas as such (Gauba, PDF pp.33-34).
- ⚠️ **Mannheim's total/particular distinction and relationalism (supplementary vocabulary, no specific page anchor claimed):** Mannheim's own work further distinguishes a "total" conception of ideology (applied reflexively to all thought, including one's own) from a narrower "particular" conception (which accuses only a rival of distortion), and frames the search for more objective knowledge as "relational" — perspective-bound but not simply arbitrary — rather than fully relativistic. This finer terminology is well established in secondary literature on Mannheim but is not independently confirmed here against the specific Gauba page range used for this file, so no page anchor is given for it; treat it as ⚠️ analytical enrichment only.
- ⚠️ **Intelligentsia caution:** Mannheim's proposed route to a more objective synthesis relies on a "free-floating" stratum of intellectuals capable of moving between class perspectives; the recorded criticism is that this risks confusing the social origin of knowledge with its validity and can slide into an unaccountable claim to expert authority (Gauba, PDF p.34). Any answer using Mannheim should end with this caution rather than treating his synthesis as an unproblematic solution.

### SESSION 7 — The End-of-Ideology Proponents

#### 13. End-of-ideology proponents, technocratic managerialism, and their critics
- ✅ **Daniel Bell:** read a mid-century Western convergence around the welfare state, decentralized power, mixed economy and pluralism as evidence that the age of sharp ideological (Left-Right) conflict had ended (Gauba, PDF pp.37-38).
- ✅ **Ralph Dahrendorf:** argued post-capitalist society had institutionally isolated industrial (workplace) conflict from wider political conflict, weakening the applicability of a Marx-style total-conflict framework to Western societies (Gauba, PDF p.37).
- ✅ **Seymour Martin Lipset:** described Western liberal democracy as approximating "the good society in operation," which narrowed the practical stakes of Left-Right doctrinal dispute in his account (Gauba, PDF pp.37-38).
- ✅ **W.W. Rostow:** cast economic development as a universal, stage-based process not determined by which political ideology a society professed (Gauba, PDF p.38).
- ✅ **J.K. Galbraith — technocratic managerialism explained:** argued industrial societies were converging through centralization, bureaucratization and technocratization, with real power shifting away from ideological political leadership toward a bureaucratic-technocratic managerial elite. This is the chapter's clearest statement of technocratic managerialism: policy is increasingly justified through technical/administrative expertise and organizational efficiency rather than through open ideological argument, so that debate appears to move "beyond ideology" even though a particular (managerial) way of allocating power is being naturalized (Gauba, PDF p.38).
- ✅ **Objections — Titmuss, C. Wright Mills, Macpherson and MacIntyre:** challenge the end-of-ideology writers by pointing, respectively, to persistent monopolistic/concentrated power, cultural and civic deprivation beneath material affluence, status-quo complacency dressed as scientific neutrality, and the deeper charge that the end-of-ideology thesis is itself an ideology defending existing arrangements (Gauba, PDF p.39).
- ⚠️ Reading proponents and objectors together: technocratic managerialism does not remove ideology from politics; it relocates ideological argument into apparently neutral administrative/technical language. This is exactly why the critics call the "end of ideology" thesis self-refuting — a claim of neutrality can itself defend a particular distribution of power.

#### 15. Doctrine architecture: premises, mechanism, conclusion
- ✅ **Premises:** (a) ideology is a group-accepted idea-set used to justify or denounce a distribution of power (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29); (b) theory critically tests validity while ideology asserts from a committed standpoint (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30); (c) ideology can also mean a "science of ideas" studying how ideas form, distort and are purified (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31).
- ✅ **Mechanism:** because ideology's action-orientation makes it useful for mobilization but prone to distortion (Marx's false consciousness, Lukács's class-situated consciousness, Mannheim's total conception), and because a closed ideological system can escalate into totalitarian monopoly over truth (Popper/Arendt), a theory of ideology must (i) explain the distortion/mobilization mechanism, (ii) mark the threshold at which ideology becomes totalitarian closure, and (iii) test any claim that ideology has become historically unnecessary (end-of-ideology) against its own critics (§13).
- ✅ **Conclusion:** ideology neither disappears nor deserves uncritical trust; it remains a permanent feature of politics whose distortions and mobilizing uses must be continuously tested by theory, and even a "post-ideological," technocratic-managerial politics can smuggle in an ideology of its own (Gauba, PDF pp.36, 39).

#### 17. Objection → reply chains
- ⚠️ **Chain 1 — Objection:** "Marx says ideology is false consciousness, so all ideology is simply false and should be discarded." **Reply:** Lenin's proletarian ideology and Lukács's class consciousness show ideology can also organize legitimate emancipatory politics, and Gauba explicitly notes that oppressed groups' ideologies may express legitimate claims (Gauba, PDF p.36); the accurate generalisation is that ideology is action-oriented belief, not automatically falsehood.
- ⚠️ **Chain 2 — Objection:** "The end-of-ideology thesis proves advanced societies have moved beyond ideological conflict, so ideology is no longer analytically relevant." **Reply:** Titmuss, Wright Mills, Macpherson and MacIntyre show the thesis itself can defend a status quo of concentrated power and civic deprivation dressed as scientific/technocratic neutrality — the "end of ideology" claim can be ideological in exactly the sense it denies (Gauba, PDF p.39).
- ⚠️ **Chain 3 — Objection:** "Mannheim's sociology of knowledge proves objective political truth is impossible." **Reply:** Mannheim still seeks a synthetic, relationally more objective knowledge through reflective, cross-perspective intellectual work; his aim is better-grounded objectivity, not surrender to relativism, though the recorded criticism is that this risks confusing origin with validity (Gauba, PDF p.34).

**Introduction:** Ideology has a dual meaning: it may denote an organised body of political ideas, and it may denote inquiry into how ideas arise, become distorted and serve social interests.

**Introduction:** Popper and Arendt analyse the point at which ideology ceases to be one contestable political perspective and becomes an instrument of total domination.

**Core analysis:** Popper defends the open society, where institutions permit criticism, error correction and piecemeal reform. **Conclusion:** Both expose the danger of monopoly truth, but the lasting lesson is not ideology-free politics; it is the institutional protection of criticism, plurality and revisability.

**Introduction:** Technocratic managerialism presents public decisions as questions for administrators and experts rather than conflicts among rival social purposes.

**Core analysis:** Galbraith's account helps explain why ideology may appear to decline: complex organisations transfer power to specialised managerial and technical elites, and policy is justified through efficiency, expertise and problem-solving. ### 2. Scope, assumptions and conceptual boundary
- ✅ The chapter works on two tracks at once: ideology as a practical, action-oriented set of ideas and ideology as a reflective investigation into the formation and distortion of ideas (Gauba, PDF pp.28, 30-31).
- ✅ The advanced debate is historically layered: Marx and Lenin belong to the critique of class ideology; Popper and Arendt shift the focus toward totalitarian rule; Bell and others reframe the issue around industrial convergence (Gauba, PDF pp.32-39).
- ⚠️ The conceptual boundary to maintain is this: a theory can be historically located without becoming ideological, because validity and origin are distinct questions.
- ⚠️ The end-of-ideology thesis must be treated as a mid-twentieth-century explanatory claim about affluent industrial societies, not as a timeless verdict on politics.

### 3. Direct-book argument reconstruction
1. ✅ Gauba begins by showing ideology as group-accepted truth that justifies power arrangements and mobilizes adherents to defend or transform them (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29).
2. ✅ He then distinguishes theory from ideology by making critical examination the hallmark of theory and partisan commitment the hallmark of ideology (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30).
3. ✅ The chapter recovers Tracy's original sense of ideology as a science of ideas and links it to Bacon's concern with distorted knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31).
4. ✅ Marx turns ideology into false consciousness rooted in the material structure of society; Lenin neutralizes the term enough to speak of proletarian ideology; Lukacs deepens the class-consciousness line (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33).
5. ✅ Mannheim generalizes the sociology of knowledge, treats both ideology and utopia as forms of false consciousness, and proposes intellectually mediated synthesis as a route to more objective knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.33-34).
6. ✅ Popper and Arendt connect ideology with totalitarian closure, monopoly over truth, mass mobilization and terror (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36).
7. ✅ Bell, Dahrendorf, Lipset, Rostow and Galbraith argue in different ways that advanced industrial development weakens classical ideological conflict, but Gauba closes with major criticisms of this thesis and declines to endorse it as final truth (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39).

### 7. Internal debates and criticisms
- ✅ Marx versus Lenin: Marx places ideology primarily on the side of distortion and domination, whereas Lenin insists that proletarian politics also needs ideology as organized consciousness (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33).
- ✅ Lenin versus Lukacs: both widen the use of ideology, but Lukacs adds the stronger thesis that consciousness is always class consciousness and that proletariat can reach a universal standpoint (Gauba, PDF p.33).
- ✅ Mannheim's solution is ambitious but unstable: Gauba records the criticism that it confuses the social origin of knowledge with the validity of knowledge and risks rule by social scientists (Gauba, PDF p.34).
- ✅ Popper and Arendt do not merely dislike ideology as rhetoric; they fear ideology fused with the state's claim to possess final truth and to mobilize masses accordingly (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36).
- ✅ Titmuss, C. Wright Mills, Macpherson and MacIntyre challenge end-of-ideology writers by pointing to monopolistic power, cultural deprivation, status-quo complacency and the possibility that end-of-ideology is itself an ideology (Gauba, PDF p.39).
- ⚠️ The deepest internal tension is that ideology can be both emancipatory and distorting; any answer that treats it as only one of these misses the chapter's architecture.

| Main concern | ✅ Class power, consciousness and domination (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33). | ✅ Social conditioning of all knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.33-34). | ✅ Closed truth-claims and total domination (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). | ✅ Industrial convergence and declining grand conflict (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39). |
| Meaning of ideology | ✅ Distortion for Marx; broadened class consciousness for Lenin/Lukacs (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33). | ✅ Conservation of status quo, contrasted with utopia (Gauba, PDF p.34). | ✅ Mobilizing doctrine of totalitarian rule (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). | ✅ A category said to be fading in affluent industrial orders (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39). |
| View of truth | ✅ Distorted by class domination, but revolutionary consciousness can expose it (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33). | ✅ Truth is perspectival but synthetic common knowledge is still sought (Gauba, PDF p.34). | ✅ Truth requires openness, criticism and no final monopoly claim (Gauba, PDF p.35). | ⚠️ Truth is often displaced by consensus around welfare capitalism and technocratic development. |
| Political implication | ✅ Critique ruling ideology or arm emancipatory struggle (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33, 36). | ✅ Build reflexive social science, though not unchecked technocracy (Gauba, PDF p.34). | ✅ Defend openness, criticism and limits on state-totalization (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). | ✅ Downplay revolutionary ideological conflict in advanced societies (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39). |
| Best exam caution | ⚠️ Do not equate all Marxian usage of ideology with simple falsehood. | ⚠️ Do not treat sociology of knowledge as proof that truth is impossible. | ⚠️ Do not universalize the totalitarian model to all ideological politics. | ⚠️ Do not mistake a historical thesis for an established contemporary fact. |

### 9. Application without current-affairs dependence
- ⚠️ In a labour-capital conflict, a Marxian lens asks whose interests a doctrine of "freedom of contract" really protects; a theory lens then tests whether that claim is normatively defensible.
- ⚠️ In an anti-colonial movement, ideology may unify dispersed people around a cause; theory must still ask whether the movement's goals and methods withstand criticism.
- ⚠️ In a one-party regime that claims to embody history's final truth, Popper-Arendt questions become central: is criticism allowed, can policy be revised, and is all life being directed toward one imposed end?
- ⚠️ In an affluent democracy with welfare protections, end-of-ideology questions ask whether ideological conflict has really disappeared or merely changed form.

### ONE-PAGE CONCEPT GRID

| Concept / thinker | Exam-ready formulation |
|---|---|
| Ideology (set of ideas) | A set of ideas accepted as true by a group, party or nation without further examination, often used to justify or denounce a social or political arrangement (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29). |
| Ideology (science of ideas) | A study of how ideas are formed, distorted and purified so that true knowledge may be separated from false knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31). |
| Obscurantism | A deliberate effort to keep things obscure so that people do not know the truth (Gauba, PDF p.29). |
| Sociology of knowledge | Inquiry into how knowledge is socially determined, conditioned or distorted; Gauba links the term especially with Mannheim (Gauba, PDF p.31). |
| Critical theory | A stream of thought that studies society from the angle of deviation from rational human emancipation, not by simply copying natural-science paradigms (Gauba, PDF p.31). |
| Utopia | A fascinating but unrealizable vision or set of principles used by Mannheim to contrast with ideology (Gauba, PDF p.34). |
| Totalitarianism | A regime-form seeking to direct all aspects of life toward state-defined goals and suppress criticism or alternative aims (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36). |
| Open society | A society with free flow of information, criticism, policy revision and no monopoly claim to truth (Gauba, PDF p.35). |
| Ideology vs theory | Ideology is interested, action-oriented and justificatory; theory is disinterested, critical and validity-testing (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30). |
| Set of ideas vs science of ideas | The first sense mobilizes adherents; the second sense studies how ideas arise and become distorted (Gauba, PDF pp.28, 30-31). |
| Closed mind vs open mind | Gauba's dimensions table opposes ideology-as-faith to science-of-ideas as critical examination and openness to questioning authority (Gauba, PDF p.32). |
| Ideology vs utopia | Mannheim treats both as forms of false consciousness: ideology conserves the status quo, utopia energizes change through unrealizable projection (Gauba, PDF p.34). |

### CORE REVISION SPINE

- UPSC repeatedly tests whether a student can distinguish theory from ideology, and neutral analysis from partisan justification; this chapter provides that distinction directly (Gauba, PDF pp.29-31).
- The so-called end of ideology is presented by Gauba as a mid-twentieth-century Western thesis, not as an unquestionable law of political development (Gauba, PDF pp.36-39).
- Gauba first explains ideology as action-oriented group belief used to justify power, attack opponents, motivate sacrifice or spur social transformation (Gauba, PDF pp.28-29).
- He then distinguishes political theory from ideology by insisting that theory critically evaluates claims, whereas ideology pursues partisan ends from a committed standpoint (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30).
- Next, he returns to the older meaning of ideology as science of ideas, beginning with Destutt de Tracy and the wider scientific concern with valid versus distorted knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.30-31).
- Marx, Lukacs and Mannheim are then introduced as major analysts of ideology as distortion, class consciousness or social conditioning of knowledge (Gauba, PDF pp.32-34).
- Popper and Arendt redirect the issue toward totalitarianism, showing how ideology can become a closed, state-backed claim to absolute truth (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36).
- The chapter closes with the end-of-ideology debate, where Bell, Dahrendorf, Lipset, Rostow and Galbraith present historically situated claims about affluent industrial societies and the waning of sharp ideological division (Gauba, PDF pp.37-39).
- The deeper spine is this: ideology can be both a motivating framework and an object of criticism, so the task of theory is neither blind celebration nor lazy dismissal.
- Nationalism may mobilize sacrifice for freedom, whereas communalism may mobilize hatred and destruction; Gauba uses these contrasts to show ideology's action-orientation (Gauba, PDF p.29).
- Environmentalism is given as an example of an ideology that may challenge destructive arrangements rather than merely defend vested interests (Gauba, PDF p.29).
- A clean classroom example is this: an ideology says "our model alone is just"; political theory asks what evidence, concepts and moral grounds support or weaken that claim.

### HIGH-RISK TRAPS

- Trap repair: Ideology always means falsehood. -> Gauba criticizes this blanket view and says oppressed groups also have ideologies that may express legitimate claims and emancipatory movements (Gauba, PDF p.36).
- Trap repair: Theory and ideology are identical because both use words like liberalism or socialism. -> Theory critically evaluates; ideology justifies or mobilizes from commitment (Gauba, PDF pp.29-30).
- Trap repair: End of ideology is an established contemporary fact. -> In Gauba it is a historically situated thesis raised in the mid-1950s and 1960s about industrial Western societies (Gauba, PDF pp.36-39).
- Trap repair: Popper and Arendt say all politics must be ideology-free. -> Their target is ideology as a totalizing closed claim to truth in totalitarian systems (Gauba, PDF pp.35-36).
- Trap repair: Marx and Lenin use ideology in exactly the same sense. -> Marx emphasizes false consciousness tied to domination; Lenin allows a proletarian ideology for socialist struggle (Gauba, PDF pp.32-33).
