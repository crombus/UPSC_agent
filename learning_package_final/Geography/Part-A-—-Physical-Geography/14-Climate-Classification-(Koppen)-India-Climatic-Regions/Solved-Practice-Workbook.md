### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why Köppen's classification is empirical and vegetation-linked rather than genetic. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** Köppen classifies long-term observed temperature and precipitation using thresholds selected to approximate broad vegetation limits; the system describes **what** climatic regime occurs, not **why** circulation produces it. Tropical A needs sufficiently warm coolest months, while B marks an aridity threshold and E a very cold warmest month. Draw a station-to-code arrow: rainfall/temperature normals → threshold → mapped region → broad vegetation correspondence. At the same latitude the wet windward Western Ghats and drier Deccan interior may occupy different classes because monsoon winds and relief intervene. A code maps this contrast reproducibly but does not itself explain the rain shadow. Genetic schemes start instead with air masses, circulation and uplift. Thus Köppen is useful for comparison and broad habitat inference, but vegetation does not always match one code, particularly where irrigation and land use alter cover.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Decode the logic of the letters in Af, Am, Aw, BW, BS and Cwa. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** The first capital sets the major group: A denotes tropical climates, B dry climates, C warm-temperate climates. Within tropical A, **Af** lacks a dry season, **Am** has monsoonal rain and a shorter dry interval, and **Aw** has a dry winter; compare a wet Western Ghats tract with the seasonally dry peninsular interior. ### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the threshold and boundary problems in Köppen classification. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** Köppen's temperature–precipitation thresholds are reproducible and permit a world climate map that broadly tracks vegetation; a station can be reclassified transparently from its long-term normals. But a sharp code line on a map crosses real gradients. The C/D coldest-month boundary, for instance, has −3°C and 0°C conventions; marginal stations can change letter without any physical climate shift. Group B depends on rainfall and temperature-related dryness, so an area may switch class with warming even when rainfall is relatively stable. India illustrates a causal blind spot: windward Western Ghats are wet and leeward Deccan dry at similar latitude, yet the code labels the two outcomes without explaining monsoon-orographic uplift. A gridded map also depends on station coverage, spatial resolution, baseline years and altitude; high Himalayan valleys are hard to reduce to one H label. Thornthwaite's precipitation–potential-evapotranspiration balance is better targeted to crop-water needs, and a genetic circulation approach better explains why rainfall falls where it does. Use Köppen as a first descriptive layer, state threshold conventions and map provenance, and supplement it with dynamic relief and risk evidence rather than pretending the boundary is a permanent physical wall.

**Why this earns marks:** The model decodes the climatic-map demand, names Indian zones and their causal controls, marks the key threshold or water-balance qualification and reaches an applied conclusion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the controls that create India's climatic diversity without relying on a code list. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** India's climatic map emerges from unequal heating, seasonal winds, mountain barriers and distance from sea, not a list of labels. During the southwest monsoon, moist onshore air rises on the Western Ghats, wetting the seaward Kerala/Konkan slope, while descent leaves parts of the leeward Deccan comparatively dry: sketch the same latitude on either side of the ridge with ascending-rain and descending-dry arrows. Rajasthan's inland location, weak moisture penetration and subtropical subsidence favour aridity; its yearly water balance cannot be read from latitude alone. The Bay of Bengal branch and Himalayan barrier shape wet northeast and northern-plain summer conditions, while high altitude creates cold Himalayan belts adjacent to much warmer lowlands. Tamil Nadu's coast receives important northeast-monsoon rain, shifting its seasonal maximum relative to the west coast. Sea proximity moderates temperature compared with continental interiors; in winter, north Indian plains cool substantially while higher terrain experiences vertical zonation. Köppen letters can record these contrasts but do not cause them. The mapped boundary is sensitive to baseline, relief resolution and changing monsoon regimes, so local planning also needs rainfall seasonality, water demand and hazard exposure.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Compare Köppen, Trewartha and Thornthwaite as tools for classifying Indian climate. Answer in about 300 words.

**Independent model answer:** Köppen's empirical station-based temperature and precipitation thresholds yield reproducible, vegetation-linked A/B/C/D/E groups; H is a later highland convention. The Indian map distinguishes wet tropical West Coast tracts (Am), dry-winter peninsular interior (Aw), semi-arid Deccan rain-shadow (BSh), Rajasthan desert (BWh), north Indian plains (Cw variants) and high Himalayan cold zones. Its strength is shared language for large-scale comparison; its weakness is that the code does not explain monsoon winds or rainfall barriers, and abrupt class lines overstate gradual transitions. Trewartha modifies temperature boundaries and recognises a more explicit midlatitude grouping; it can correct some over-broad Köppen classes but remains empirical, with code details that must not be conflated between schemes. Thornthwaite compares precipitation with potential evapotranspiration in a moisture index; it is useful for Indian drought and irrigation planning because crop-water stress depends on demand, not rainfall totals alone. But its data requirements are greater, and no moisture class predicts a specific farmer's losses without soil, crop and exposure information. On a section across the Western Ghats, Köppen names humid slope versus steppe-like rain shadow; a genetic monsoon/uplift explanation supplies causation, while Thornthwaite sharpens the water-balance implication. Use complementary descriptive, water-balance and causal layers, with stated data period and spatial scale.

**Why this earns marks:** The model decodes the climatic-map demand, names Indian zones and their causal controls, marks the key threshold or water-balance qualification and reaches an applied conclusion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Design a multi-layer climatic regionalisation for Indian planning under climate variability. Answer in about 300 words.

**Independent model answer:** Begin with a reproducible long-term station/gridded record of monthly temperature and rain, documenting baseline years, gap-filling, altitude and map resolution. Apply one stated Köppen threshold convention to make comparable climate-zone boundaries, then add water-balance estimates of precipitation minus potential evapotranspiration in Thornthwaite's spirit. Overlay causal features: monsoon wind exposure, Western Ghats/Himalayan orography, distance to sea and seasonal rainfall distribution. A transect from wet Kerala windward slopes across the leeward Deccan to arid northwestern Rajasthan illustrates why one rainfall number cannot characterise a crop zone. Add an independent hazard layer, such as the IMD climate hazards/vulnerability atlas, and population, crops, drainage and irrigation capacity: an Aw label is not a flood-loss forecast, nor is BSh an exact drought risk for each farm. For climate change, compare like-for-like baselines and scenarios before declaring migration of B or H boundaries; a change in grid scale or C/D threshold must not be misreported as climate change. Validate the map against observed local water stress, heat exposure and seasonal crop calendars, then periodically update it and display uncertainty at transition belts. The output should guide crop choices and water infrastructure while protecting high-risk settlements, not reduce a dynamic district to an immutable single-letter designation.
