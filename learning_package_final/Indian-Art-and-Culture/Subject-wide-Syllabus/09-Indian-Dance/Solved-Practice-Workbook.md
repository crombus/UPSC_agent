### Q1. Which statement about Natya Shastra is safest?

**Explanation:** A is correct because the core source defines Natya Veda through paathya, abhinaya, geet and rasa. B is too narrow, C confuses a classical text with a modern institution, and D converts a textual framework into an unsound continuity claim.

### DIRECT MAINS PYQS WITH MODEL ANSWERS

#### PYQ-M1. Discuss the 'Tandava' dance as recorded in early Indian inscriptions. (CSE 2013)

**Model solution**

Tandava should not be reduced to a crude image of violence. In the Indian dance tradition, Tandava denotes the vigorous and rhythmic aspect of dance, contrasted with the graceful-emotive field of Lasya. Early inscriptional and temple traditions connect this vigorous mode especially with Shiva, whose Nataraja image later becomes the most recognisable civilisational emblem of cosmic rhythm.

In answer-writing, the safer route is not to hunt unsourced inscription names but to explain what the tradition records. Tandava stands for energy, dynamic body movement, rhythmic emphasis and performative force. That is why later forms such as Perini Sivathandavam, the warrior dance associated with Kakatiya memory and Shiva worship, are useful explanatory bridges: they show how the vigorous side of dance became ritualised and historically embodied.

The best conclusion is that Tandava is an interpretive category of movement in Indian dance history. It belongs to the Natya Shastra performance vocabulary and later sculpture-dance iconography, and it should be explained through energy, rhythm and Shiva-linked performative symbolism rather than through sensationalism.

#### PYQ-M2. Describe the Bagurumba folk dance. (CSE 2012)

**Model solution**

Bagurumba is a well-known folk dance of Assam's Bodo community. It is nature-themed and is performed with music using instruments such as the sifung, kham and serja. Unlike a classical form identified through fixed textual grammar, Bagurumba is rooted in community performance and regional ecology.

Its importance lies in what it preserves. The dance translates landscape, rhythm and local social memory into movement. That is why the safest way to describe it is through community and theme rather than by forcing it into the framework of a classical dance form. In a GS-I answer, Bagurumba can be used to show that Indian dance history is not exhausted by elite classical canons.

Thus Bagurumba is best described as a Bodo folk dance of Assam in which nature, community and music together shape performance.

#### PYQ-M4. List the salient features of the important folk dances of central India or North Eastern India. (CSE 2009)

**Model solution**

The salient features of folk dances of central and North-Eastern India are best organised through region, community and occasion.

**Central India**
- **Gaur Muria (Chhattisgarh):** ritual dance of the Muria tribes of Bastar; imitates the bison; group performance by men and women.
- **Raut Nacha (Chhattisgarh):** associated with the Yadav community and especially visible around Diwali.
- **Rai Nritya (Madhya Pradesh):** Bundelkhand dance associated with the Bedia tribe, often described as peacock-like.
- **Jawara / Matki (Madhya Pradesh):** harvest, festival and balancing-performance settings.

**North-East**
- **Bihu (Assam):** lively group dance with brisk steps and colourful dress.
- **Bagurumba (Assam):** Bodo nature-themed dance.
- **Hojagiri (Tripura):** lower-body movement with balancing skill during Lakshmi Puja.
- **Garia / Lebang Boomani (Tripura):** sowing and harvest-cycle associations.
- **Cheraw (Mizoram):** bamboo-stick dance.
- **Thang-Ta (Manipur):** martial performance based on sword and spear.

The comparative conclusion is that these forms preserve labour, harvest, ritual and martial memory rather than imitating classical dance.

#### PYQ-M5. Write about Perini Sivathandavam. (CSE 2008)

**Model solution**

Perini Sivathandavam is a martial dance tradition associated with Telangana and Kakatiya memory. It is performed in honour of Lord Shiva and is often described as a warrior's dance. Nitin also links it to sculptural depiction near the garbha gudi of the Ramappa Temple at Warangal, which gives the form a useful architecture-dance bridge.

Historically, its importance lies in the performative use of vigour. Perini carries the Tandava side of dance in a distinctly martial direction: the body becomes a medium of mobilisation, devotion and heroic energy. This separates it from lyrical lasya-oriented forms.

Therefore, Perini Sivathandavam is best understood as a Shiva-linked, warrior-oriented performative memory of the Deccan, not merely as an energetic folk routine.

#### PYQ-M6. Write a short note on Kathakali. (CSE 1992)

**Model solution**

Kathakali is Kerala's celebrated dance-drama tradition. It grew out of the performance world of Ramanattam and Krishnattam and retains close links with Koodiyattam and martial performance. The very name joins `katha` (story) and `kali` (play or performance).

Its chief features are elaborate character-specific make-up and headgear, generally all-male troupe performance, Attakkatha text, Manipravalam language, minimal props, and powerful use of eyes, eyebrows and hand gestures for representing the rasas. Chenda and maddala drums create the dramatic frame. It is often staged in open-air or temple-premises settings with a brass lamp.

Kathakali thus represents the fullest dance-drama integration within the Indian classical spectrum.

#### PYQ-M7. Write a short note on the Manipuri dance. (CSE 1991)

**Model solution**

Manipuri is the major classical dance tradition of Manipur and is deeply tied to Vaishnava devotional performance. Krishna and Ras Leela form its recurring thematic centre, while the pung drum and the restrained emotional field of performance sharply identify the form.

The dance is known for devotional emphasis rather than overt sensuality. Facial display is comparatively restrained, the face may be veiled, lasya is prominent, and graceful hand-knee movement is central. Nagabandha is one of its important posture-markers. Historically, Raja Bhag Chandra's revival and Tagore's later introduction of the form at Santiniketan are major milestones.

Manipuri is therefore best described as a devotional classical form where rhythm, grace and Krishna-centred enactment are fused within a distinct regional idiom.

#### PYQ-M8. What are the main forms of classical dance in India? Indicate two features of each. (CSE 1988)

**Model solution**

The main classical dance forms in the standard Sangeet Natak Akademi list are Bharatanatyam, Kuchipudi, Kathakali, Mohiniyattam, Kathak, Odissi, Manipuri and Sattriya.

- **Bharatanatyam:** Sadir-devadasi origin; Tanjore Quartet recital order and adavu-based grammar.
- **Kuchipudi:** Kuchelapuram village association; Tarangam and dance-drama line.
- **Kathakali:** Attakkatha and elaborate vesham; eye-brow-led dance-drama.
- **Mohiniyattam:** Kasavu costume; gentle lasya-oriented footwork.
- **Kathak:** tatkar and pirouettes; storytelling and gharana history.
- **Odissi:** tribhanga and chowk; mahari-gotipua continuity and sculptural quality.
- **Manipuri:** Ras Leela and pung; devotional restraint.
- **Sattriya:** sattra-monastic setting; Borgeet and Ankia Naat connection.

A high-scoring answer closes by warning that SNA classical status is distinct from UNESCO inscription or from a Ministry list including Chhau.

## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE WITH MODEL SOLUTIONS

### Original Q1. Explain the contribution of Natya Shastra to Indian dance. Why is terminological precision central to scoring in this topic? (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model solution**

Natya Shastra gives Indian dance its foundational grammar by treating performance as a composite of text, enactment, music and rasa. That is why dance is analysed through categories rather than through vague appreciation. It provides the conceptual frame within which later traditions work.

Terminological precision is central because UPSC repeatedly tests close distinctions. **Nritta** is pure rhythmic movement, **Nritya** is expressive dance, and **Natya** is dramatic enactment. **Lasya** and **Tandava** are contrasting movement emphases, not rigid gender categories. **Hasta** differs from **karana**, and the famous number **108** belongs to the latter.

Without this precision, a candidate cannot distinguish Bharatanatyam from Kuchipudi, Odissi from Manipuri, or SNA status from UNESCO safeguarding. Therefore Natya Shastra contributes not only an ancient text but also the vocabulary that makes answer-writing accurate.

### Original Q2. Bharatanatyam should be written as continuity plus reconstruction rather than as either timeless survival or modern invention. Discuss. (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model solution**

Bharatanatyam is best understood as a reconfigured continuity. Its repertoire grows out of **Sadir / Dashiattam**, the performance of temple dancers or devadasis in Tamil Nadu. This gives the form a deep pre-modern base.

Yet its modern public life emerged only after disruption. The decline of the devadasi system weakened the hereditary performance world, after which figures such as **E. Krishna Iyer** and **Rukmini Devi Arundale** worked to revive and restage the form. Meanwhile the **Tanjore Quartet** had already codified the recital order, giving the repertoire a structured performance spine.

Thus Bharatanatyam is neither a pure museum-survival nor a twentieth-century invention. Continuity lies in repertoire, movement grammar and transmission; reconstruction lies in patronage, public stage, institutional prestige and social ownership.

### Original Q3. Compare Kathak and Kuchipudi through origin, technique and performance ecology. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model solution**

Kathak and Kuchipudi are both classical forms, but their performance ecologies are markedly different.

**Origin:** Kathak derives from the storytelling line of **Kathikas** and from the Raslila world of North India. Later, Mughal courts reshaped its presentation and helped generate distinct gharanas. Kuchipudi, by contrast, is tied to **Kuchelapuram** village in Andhra Pradesh and to travelling actors or **Kusselavas**, later shaped through Siddhendra Yogi and Bhagavata-centred dance-drama.

**Technique:** Kathak is identified by **tatkar**, pirouettes, padhant and rhythmic interplay such as **jugalbandi**. Kuchipudi shares classical grammar but foregrounds dance-drama, occasional dialogue and signature virtuosity through **Tarangam** on the brass plate.

**Performance ecology:** Kathak moves through temple-storytelling, court patronage and gharana transmission within the Hindustani performance world. Kuchipudi remains more clearly linked to Telugu narrative enactment, Vaishnava themes and Carnatic accompaniment.

**Conclusion:** Therefore, the safest comparison is not North versus South, but storytelling-court-lineage on one side and village-dance-drama on the other.

### Original Q4. Discuss Odissi as a meeting point of dance, sculpture and revival history. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model solution**

Odissi is one of the strongest examples of how Indian dance connects bodily grammar with sculptural imagination and historical reconstruction.

**First, dance grammar.** The form is defined by **Tribhanga** and **Chowk**, torso-led movement, expressive hand gestures and a repertory sequence moving from **Mangalacharan** to **Moksha**.

**Second, sculptural link.** Odissi is often described as **mobile sculpture** because the dancer's body creates curves, geometry and rhythmic stillness-in-motion comparable to sculptural postures. This is why the form becomes valuable in answers linking dance with Khajuraho, Konark or other temple figures.

**Third, historical continuity.** The tradition remembers **Mahari** practice, later **Gotipua** continuity and the court variant called **Nartala**. In the mid-twentieth century, figures such as **Charles Fabri** and **Indrani Rahman** contributed to wider public visibility.

**Qualification:** Sculpture proves a valued movement idiom; it does not prove unchanged named choreography.

**Conclusion:** Odissi thus stands at the junction of ritual memory, sculptural vocabulary and modern revival.

### Original Q5. Examine the difference between institutional recognition and living practice in the history of Indian dance. (20 marks, 250 words)

**Model solution**

The history of Indian dance becomes distorted when institutional recognition is confused with living practice.

**Institutional recognition** is modern and classificatory. The standard **Sangeet Natak Akademi** list identifies eight classical forms, while a separate Ministry list includes **Chhau**. UNESCO inscription, as in the case of **Garba of Gujarat** in 2023, belongs to safeguarding of living heritage, not to classical canon formation.

**Living practice**, however, is older and socially layered. Bharatanatyam's modern status emerged out of Sadir and the devadasi performance world; Odissi drew upon Mahari and Gotipua memory; Sattriya remained tied to Assamese sattras; Kathakali changed after feudal decline. A form's regional and community life therefore often long predates its institutional recognition.

This distinction matters analytically. It prevents three mistakes: treating classical status as timeless essence, turning UNESCO recognition into proof of classicality, and erasing the hereditary communities whose labour sustained the repertoire.

**Conclusion:** Recognition tells us how the state or international bodies classify and safeguard a form; living practice tells us where, by whom and through what historical changes the form actually survived.

### Original Q6. Discuss the significance of folk, ritual and martial dances in understanding Indian culture. Why should they not be treated as leftovers beside classical dance? (20 marks, 250 words)

**Model solution**

Folk, ritual and martial dances are indispensable for understanding Indian culture because they preserve local memory in embodied form.

**Folk dances** such as **Garba**, **Bihu**, **Bagurumba**, **Ghoomar** and **Kalbelia** encode festival, harvest, gendered community participation, serpent symbolism or regional social rhythm.  
**Ritual dances** such as **Theyyam** and **Padayani** embody deity, myth and temple-community relations.  
**Martial dances** such as **Chhau**, **Perini** and **Thang-Ta** preserve combat memory, discipline and heroic performance.

They should not be treated as leftovers because their grammar is structured by occasion and function. They are not "failed classical forms"; they are different performance systems. In fact, they often tell us more directly about labour, devotion, seasonal cycles and local belief than elite courtly performance does.

A mature answer also keeps modern recognition in perspective. UNESCO inscription or public festivals can widen visibility, but they do not dissolve the community base from which these forms emerged.

**Conclusion:** Indian cultural history is incomplete if classical dance alone is studied. Folk, ritual and martial forms preserve the social body of culture.
