## Attempt protocol

Write one essay in 1000-1200 words. Spend five minutes deconstructing the prompt, build six distinct dimensions, include a counterargument, and review for prompt fidelity before opening the answer book.

## Essay framework and quality rubric

### Target and planning method

The UPSC Essay paper's audited 2024 instruction asks for two essays of about
1000-1200 words each, one from each section. The 1000-1200 range is a target,
not a licence to pad. A practical, non-official workflow is:

1. **Deconstruct (5 minutes):** define the key words, identify the hidden tension,
   convert the prompt into a question, and write a provisional verdict.
2. **Map (8-10 minutes):** generate dimensions across individual, society,
   polity, economy, technology, environment, ethics, global and time scales.
3. **Select (3 minutes):** retain six or seven distinct claims. Each must have a
   mechanism, a named example, analysis and a qualification.
4. **Draft (about 65 minutes):** introduction, five or six body clusters,
   counterargument, synthesis and conclusion. Use transitions that show logic.
5. **Review (5 minutes):** check prompt fidelity, balance, examples, grammar,
   word range and whether the conclusion is earned rather than sloganised.

### Recommended 1000-1200-word architecture

| Block | Indicative words | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Introduction | 110-140 | Context, definitions, tension and qualified thesis |
| Body 1-2 | 200-240 | Individual and social consequences |
| Body 3-4 | 200-240 | Institutions, economy, polity or technology |
| Body 5-6 | 200-240 | Environment, ethics, global or intergenerational lens |
| Counterargument | 100-130 | Steelman the strongest limit; answer it |
| Conclusion | 90-120 | Synthesis, conditions and forward-looking closure |

### Paragraph engine

**Claim -> named example -> mechanism -> analysis -> qualification.** A good
example is not decoration. Explain why it supports the claim, what its limit is,
and how it returns to the prompt.

### Quality rubric (100 marks)

| Criterion | Marks | Full-credit test |
|---|---:|---|
| Prompt fidelity | 25 | Every major paragraph answers the exact proposition |
| Thesis and reasoning | 20 | One contestable, qualified thesis with visible logic |
| Multidimensional depth | 20 | Distinct scales and sectors, not repeated points |
| Examples and factual restraint | 15 | Named, relevant examples without invented statistics |
| Counterargument and synthesis | 10 | Serious opposing case refined into a condition |
| Structure and transitions | 5 | Introduction, flow, balance and conclusion are easy to follow |
| Language and originality | 5 | Clear, precise, reflective and non-template prose |

### Self-audit before submission

- Is the thesis a claim rather than a restatement?
- Does each body paragraph contain a mechanism and an example?
- Have I distinguished growth from development, access from agency, and charity
  from justice where the prompt requires it?
- Have I given the strongest counter-view a fair hearing?
- Is the conclusion a qualified answer to the prompt, not a new topic?
- Are all exact figures, quotations and attributions either verified or omitted?
