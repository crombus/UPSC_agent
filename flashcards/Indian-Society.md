### GROUP — Salient Features and Diversity of Indian Society

### Flashcard SOC-1001 — Diversity (objective presence of multiple identities)
**Front:** What is "diversity" in the sense used in Indian Society answers?
**Definition (learn):** The objective presence of several ethnic, linguistic, religious, regional or caste/tribe identities within one society. It is a descriptive fact, not a judgement about harmony or disadvantage. (✅ owner 01)
**Breakdown:**
- Layers: ethnic/ethno-linguistic, linguistic, religious, regional, caste and tribe (owner 01 §1).
- Layers overlap and are not parallel; they can cross-cut or reinforce each other (SOC-1013).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** No figure in the definition itself. Scale anchors are in SOC-1010 and SOC-1011. (✅ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1002 (plurality), SOC-1003 (disparity), SOC-1004 (marginality), SOC-1005 (unity-in-diversity), SOC-1013.
**Trap:** Diversity is not disparity. A diverse society can be equal or unequal (SOC-1003).
**Mains hook:** Start any diversity answer by separating the fact of difference from its outcome. Qualification: diversity is structural, but its social meaning depends on how identities are arranged.
**Back:** *"Diversity is the fact of many identities, not the fact of a problem."*

### Flashcard SOC-1002 — Plurality (coexistence with mutual recognition)
**Front:** How does plurality differ from diversity?
**Definition (learn):** Plurality is coexistence of multiple identities with mutual recognition and acceptance. Diversity is headcount variety; plurality adds acceptance. (✅ owner 01 §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Diversity: variety exists.
- Plurality: variety is acknowledged and respected by others.
- Owner 01 asks for "diversity and plurality with Indian examples" as a 10-mark demand family.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** No figure. Owner-only conceptual definition. (✅ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1001, SOC-1005, SOC-1006 (syncretism).
**Trap:** Equating plurality with the absence of conflict. Plurality is recognition, not the absence of tension (SOC-1005).
**Mains hook:** Distinguish diversity from plurality with two Indian instances (for example, Sufi-Bhakti shared shrines for recognition; separate religious spaces without mutual access for mere coexistence). ⚠️ the second instance is illustrative.
**Back:** *"Diversity counts heads; plurality counts respect."*

### Flashcard SOC-1003 — Disparity versus diversity (2024 Q17 distinction)
**Front:** Why must "disparity" not be answered with "diversity"?
**Definition (learn):** Disparity is unequal development outcomes between groups or regions (income, schooling, health). Diversity is the presence of different identities. A diverse society need not be disparate. (✅ owner 01 §7)
**Breakdown:**
- Diversity: presence of identities.
- Disparity: unequal outcomes between groups or regions.
- Owner 01 routes the exact 2024 Q17 distinction to Topic 14 (Regionalism), which is outside this fragment.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** Owner cites 2024 Q17 for the distinction. (✅ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1001, SOC-1004, SOC-1013.
**Trap:** A disparity question answered with cultural description scores no marks. Diagnose disparity with development indicators first.
**Mains hook:** Use disparity as the outcome term and diversity as the identity term; name which mechanism links them in the case given.
**Back:** *"Diversity is who we are; disparity is who gets what."*

### Flashcard SOC-1004 — Marginality (exclusion from opportunity, resources or voice)
**Front:** What is marginality, and why is it not automatic for a distinct group?
**Definition (learn):** Socio-economic exclusion from mainstream opportunity, resources or voice. It is often, but not always, tied to an identity group. (⚠️ owner 01 §2, analytical definition)
**Breakdown:**
- Access: schooling, health, markets.
- Assets: land, credit, employment.
- Voice: representation and decision-making.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. Conceptual card. (⚠️ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1001, SOC-1013, SOC-1014 (correlation vs causation).
**Trap:** Every minority or tribal group is not automatically marginalised. Several diverse communities are prosperous (SOC-1015).
**Mains hook:** Treat marginality as conditional on historical exclusion and access, not on cultural distinctiveness. Qualification: the mechanism, not the identity marker, is the cause.
**Back:** *"Marginality is exclusion, and exclusion is not identity."*

### Flashcard SOC-1005 — Unity-in-diversity (aspiration and observed pattern)
**Front:** What is "unity-in-diversity", and what is its limit?
**Definition (learn):** A civilisational and constitutional aspiration that difference need not produce division if integrative institutions are strong. It is an aspiration and an observed pattern, not a guarantee. (⚠️ owner 01 §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Integrative mechanisms: syncretic traditions, shared festivals, common schools, markets, elections, public transport (owner 01 §4).
- Marginalising pressures: remoteness, caste stigma, minority-concentration districts with weak infrastructure.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None beyond owner text. (⚠️ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1006, SOC-1007, SOC-1012, SOC-1013.
**Trap:** Unity-in-diversity does not mean conflict never occurs. Communal and regional tensions coexist with syncretism (owner 01 §7).
**Mains hook:** Use unity-in-diversity as an aspiration to test, not a settled fact. Verdict: cohesion is produced by cross-cutting identities plus institutions that prevent difference hardening into deprivation.
**Back:** *"Unity is a practice to keep up, not a fact to assume."*

### Flashcard SOC-1006 — Syncretism
**Front:** What is syncretism, with an Indian example?
**Definition (learn):** The blending of practices across religious or cultural traditions into a shared composite form. (✅ owner 01 §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Examples named in owner: Sufi-Bhakti idioms and shared shrine worship.
- Shared local deities and regional festivals (Onam, Bihu, Pongal) are listed as composite practices (owner 01 §4).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None beyond owner text. (✅ owner 01)
**Links:** SOC-1005, SOC-1007, Secularism owner 15 (cross-owner).
**Trap:** Syncretism is practice-level blending. It is not formal state recognition of several religions, and it does not remove sectarian boundaries.
**Mains hook:** Cite syncretism as the lived evidence of plurality. Qualify: it is local and can be eroded by communal mobilisation.
**Back:** *"Shared shrine, shared song, shared saint."*

### Flashcard SOC-1007 — Composite culture versus multiculturalism
**Front:** How do "composite culture" and "multiculturalism" differ?
**Definition (learn):** Composite culture is a regional or national culture formed through long interaction among groups, not the dominance of one. Multiculturalism is a family of theories and institutions for managing difference. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Composite culture: interwoven practices in particular settings.
- Multiculturalism: institutional management of difference, not simply "separate but tolerated".
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced)
**Links:** SOC-1005, SOC-1006.
**Trap:** Neither label proves uniform social mixing across regions.
**Mains hook:** Use composite culture for historical depth and multiculturalism for policy design. Qualify that neither proves equal power.
**Back:** *"Composite is what grew together; multicultural is what is managed."*

- Dravidian: mainly South.
- Austroasiatic: including Munda languages such as Santali (⚠️ example, memory).

**Key facts / Dates / Data:** Owner has no speaker-share figures. (✅ owner 01)

### Flashcard SOC-1013 — Cross-cutting versus reinforcing cleavages
**Front:** What is the difference between cross-cutting and reinforcing cleavages?
**Definition (learn):** Cross-cutting cleavages: divisions on one axis do not align with divisions on another, so disadvantage on one axis is often offset on another, reducing conflict intensity. Reinforcing cleavages: multiple disadvantages (remoteness, low literacy, minority status, poverty) stack on the same group. (✅ owner 01 advanced §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Cross-cutting example: a poor upper-caste household; a prosperous linguistic-minority trading community.
- Reinforcing example: a remote Scheduled Tribe household that is also low-literacy and asset-poor (owner 01 advanced E1 PVTG case).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. Analytical framework (Lipset–Rokkan tradition, owner 01 advanced header). (⚠️ owner 01 advanced)
**Links:** SOC-1014, SOC-1015, SOC-1046 (PVTG), SOC-1005.
**Trap:** Reinforcing does not mean diversity causes disadvantage; diversity can be the visible marker (SOC-1014).
**Mains hook:** Use the two-column test to decide where the diversity–marginality correlation holds. Qualified verdict: holds where cleavages reinforce.
**Back:** *"Cross-cut, and the loss is shared; stack, and the loss compounds."*

### Flashcard SOC-1014 — Correlation is not causation (diversity as marker)
**Front:** Why is "diversity causes marginality" a category error?
**Definition (learn):** Correlation between a diversity marker and deprivation does not show that the marker causes deprivation. Historical exclusion, geographic remoteness and asset-poverty are the causal drivers; diversity is often the visible marker. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Identity endowment (inherited, not chosen).
- Historical exclusion layer.
- Geographic-infrastructural layer.
- Compounding point, then counter-case (owner 01 advanced §3).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced)
**Links:** SOC-1013, SOC-1015, SOC-1004.
**Trap:** Aggregation masks which sub-group is reinforced-marginal (owner 01 advanced §6).
**Mains hook:** Diagnose the exclusion mechanism; policy that manages identity while leaving exclusion intact reproduces the correlation (owner 01 advanced verdict).
**Back:** *"The marker shows the wound; exclusion made it."*

### Flashcard SOC-1017 — Recognition versus redistribution (Fraser lens)
**Front:** How is the recognition versus redistribution distinction used in Indian Society answers?
**Definition (learn):** Recognition addresses disrespect for identity and culture; redistribution addresses unequal resources and opportunity. A group may need one, the other, or both. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced §2 uses Nancy Fraser's distinction as an analytical lens, not as constitutional doctrine)
**Breakdown:**
- Recognition-only example: declaring a festival a holiday, naming an institution after a community icon.
- Redistribution example: land, credit, education access.
- Policy fork: recognition alone leaves resource gaps; redistribution alone can leave identity grievance.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (⚠️ owner 01 advanced)
**Links:** SOC-1013, SOC-1041 (three tribal policy positions), SOC-1117 (Ambedkar).
**Trap:** Recognition measures are not redistribution. Conflating them under-delivers on the resource gap.
**Mains hook:** Diagnose which gap dominates before choosing the instrument (owner 01 T4). Qualified: the lens is a tool, not a legal category.
**Back:** *"Respect is not a rupee, and a rupee is not respect."*

**Definition (learn):** Indian diversity is not one axis but several overlapping axes; social cohesion depends on whether these axes reinforce each other into compounded disadvantage or cross-cut so that no single identity always predicts deprivation. (⚠️ owner 01 §1, analytical)

### GROUP — Caste System: Structure and Contemporary Dynamics

### Flashcard SOC-1019 — Varna versus jati
**Front:** What is the difference between varna and jati?
**Definition (learn):** Varna is the four-fold ideal-typical classical order (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra), a normative scheme. Jati is the actual local, endogamous, birth-ascribed group, of which there are thousands. (✅ owner 02 §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Varna: textual ideal; not directly observable.
- Jati: local, ranked, endogamous; the real social unit.
- Historically excluded groups sit outside the four varnas (owner 02 §1).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** No all-India jati count can be quoted as settled (owner 02 §2, §3). ✅ owner 02
**Links:** SOC-1020 (endogamy), SOC-1021 (hierarchy), SOC-1027 (Dumont), SOC-1026 (Béteille).
**Trap:** Varna and jati are not the same. Dominance is not ritual rank (SOC-1023).
**Mains hook:** Use the varna–jati level distinction to explain why caste rank varies locally.
**Back:** *"Varna is the textbook; jati is the village."*

### Flashcard SOC-1020 — Endogamy as the boundary marker
**Front:** Why is endogamy the most consistently observed feature of caste?
**Definition (learn):** The rule of marrying within one's own jati or sub-group. It is the mechanism that keeps caste boundaries intact across generations. (✅ owner 02 §2, E1)
**Breakdown:**
- Four classically cited features: endogamy, hierarchy, hereditary membership, occupational linkage (owner 02 §6).
- Operates together with gotra exogamy: marry within the jati but outside the clan (SOC-1054).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** No prevalence series in owner. (✅ owner 02)
**Links:** SOC-1019, SOC-1054 (gotra exogamy), SOC-1030 (intercaste marriage, 2024 Q9).
**Trap:** Caste endogamy and gotra exogamy do not contradict each other (SOC-1054).
**Mains hook:** Endogamy explains why occupational and ritual change has not dissolved caste. Qualify: practice varies by region, class and generation.
**Back:** *"Marry within the jati, outside the gotra: caste reproduces itself."*

### Flashcard SOC-1021 — Hierarchy, purity-pollution and hereditary membership (classic features)
**Front:** What are the classical hierarchy and hereditary features of caste, and what does "locally ranked" mean?
**Definition (learn):** Jatis are ranked by notions of purity and pollution. Membership is birth-fixed. The exact rank order varies by region. (⚠️ owner 02 §3)
**Breakdown:**
- Hierarchy: ranked status.
- Hereditary membership: birth-ascribed.
- Occupational linkage: traditional trade, weakened by urbanisation and education.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (⚠️ owner 02)
**Links:** SOC-1019, SOC-1020, SOC-1022 (occupational decoupling in E4).
**Trap:** Rank order is local, so "the upper castes" as a national ladder is misleading.
**Mains hook:** Use local ranking to explain why mobility claims are negotiated locally.
**Back:** *"Ranked by purity, fixed by birth, loosened by work."*

### Flashcard SOC-1022 — Occupational decoupling of caste
**Front:** How has the caste–occupation link changed, and what is the limit of that change?
**Definition (learn):** Many jatis were linked to a traditional occupation; urbanisation, schooling and non-farm employment have weakened this linkage sharply. (✅ owner 02 §3; E4)
**Breakdown:**
- Mechanisms: urban labour markets, schooling, non-farm work.
- Owner notes jajmani service ties were displaced by cash-wage work (see SOC-1066).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (✅ owner 02)
**Links:** SOC-1021, SOC-1066 (jajmani), SOC-1077 (rural-urban continuum).
**Trap:** Stigmatised occupations and their inheritance have not disappeared. Do not claim uniform decoupling.
**Mains hook:** This is the "fluid" half of the caste paradox.
**Back:** *"Work moved first; the marriage boundary moved last."*

### Flashcard SOC-1023 — Dominant caste (numbers, land, political power)
**Front:** Define the "dominant caste" concept and its three criteria.
**Definition (learn):** A caste that is numerically strong, holds a significant share of local land and wields political influence in a locality, whether or not it ranks highest ritually. (✅ owner 02 §2; ✅ Wikipedia: Dominant caste)
**Breakdown:**
- Numbers: considerable numerical strength.
- Land: sizeable cultivable land.
- Status/power: high place in local hierarchy and political influence (Wikipedia lists land, numbers, status; later authors add education, administrative jobs, political contacts).
- Srinivas introduced the concept in 1959.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** Coined by M.N. Srinivas; paper "The Dominant Caste in Rampura" (1959) (✅ Wikipedia: Dominant caste; ✅ owner 02 uses Rampura village, Karnataka).
**Links:** SOC-1024 (Srinivas), SOC-1025, SOC-1072 (Panchayat reservation), SOC-1073 (village power).
**Trap:** Dominant caste does not mean "upper caste" (SOC-1021). It can be middle-ranked.
**Mains hook:** Dominance rests on numbers-land-office convergence; reserved Panchayat seats contest but do not eliminate it.
**Back:** *"Numbers, land, office: the village's real top rank."*

### Flashcard SOC-1024 — M.N. Srinivas: Sanskritisation (with a source conflict)
**Front:** Who coined "Sanskritisation", and what is the process?
**Definition (learn):** The process by which a low or middle caste (or tribal or other group) changes its customs, rituals and lifestyle toward those of a high, often twice-born, caste, claiming a higher position in the hierarchy. (✅ owner 02 §2; ✅ Wikipedia: Sanskritization)
**Breakdown:**
- Upward emulation within the existing hierarchy, not a change of the hierarchy (✅ Wikipedia).
- Ambedkar earlier described similar processes (Wikipedia, 1916–17).
- Claim to status, not proof of material mobility or acceptance (owner 02).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** Popularised in the 1950s; Srinivas's 1952 book on the Kodava (Coorgs) (✅ Wikipedia). ⚠️ Owner-vs-source conflict: owner 02 says Sanskritisation was developed from Rampura fieldwork; Wikipedia ties it to the Kodava study and ties Rampura to dominant caste. Use the Wikipedia attribution for the Kodava link and flag the owner statement.
**Links:** SOC-1023 (dominant caste), SOC-1025 (Westernisation), SOC-1032 (ritual mobility vs power).
**Trap:** Sanskritisation does not automatically raise economic status. Owner 02 advanced: stalls without durable resources.
**Mains hook:** Sanskritisation is a claim to rank that can reinforce the hierarchy's logic (owner 02 E2). Qualify with the source conflict if the study site is asked.
**Back:** *"Copy the higher caste's rituals, claim its rank."*

### Flashcard SOC-1025 — Westernisation versus Sanskritisation
**Front:** How does Westernisation differ from Sanskritisation?
**Definition (learn):** Westernisation is change from sustained contact with Western (especially British) values, technology and institutions. It introduces new values such as equality and rationality, rather than emulating local hierarchy. (✅ owner 02 §2)
**Breakdown:**
- Sanskritisation: emulate existing higher caste.
- Westernisation: adopt new values.
- They can occur together, separately or in conflict in the same community (owner 02 §3).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** None. (✅ owner 02)
**Links:** SOC-1024, SOC-1026, Social-Change owner 12 (cross-owner).
**Trap:** Sanskritisation and Westernisation are not synonyms for modernisation. Modernisation implies structural, rational-institutional change (owner 02 §7).
**Mains hook:** Use the pair to separate ritual mobility from value change in any social-change answer.
**Back:** *"Sanskritisation borrows rank; Westernisation brings new values."*

### Flashcard SOC-1026 — Caste, class and power (Béteille) and caste as hierarchy versus political identity
**Front:** Why does caste, class and power need to be kept analytically distinct?
**Definition (learn):** Ritual rank (caste hierarchy), economic class and political power are three unequal resources. André Béteille's caste-class-power distinction keeps them apart. (⚠️ owner 02 advanced / Core §semantic note; not fetched)
**Breakdown:**
- Caste overlaps class but is not identical to it. Overlap loosens with urbanisation and market jobs.

### Flashcard SOC-1052 — Structural versus functional nuclearisation

### Flashcard SOC-1066 — Jajmani system (patron-client service exchange)
**Front:** What was the jajmani system?
**Definition (learn):** A village-based patron-client arrangement in which patron households (jajman) received customary services from hereditary service groups (kamin), who were compensated mainly with grain or other goods. ### Flashcard SOC-2005 — Culture of poverty (Oscar Lewis)
**Front:** What is the "culture of poverty" thesis and who proposed it?
**Definition (learn):** Oscar Lewis's thesis that persistent poverty generates values and behaviours (present orientation, low future planning, resignation) reproduced across generations, so poverty partly sustains itself.
**Breakdown:**

- Owner states fieldwork in Mexico and Puerto Rico (⚠️ conflict: owner advanced/09 §7; Wikipedia names Mexico City; Puerto Rico not confirmed on fetched page).

### Flashcard SOC-2006 — Sociological critique of culture of poverty
**Front:** Why is the culture-of-poverty thesis criticised?
**Definition (learn):** Critics argue the thesis "blames the poor" and understates structural causes such as unequal land, labour discrimination and weak services; behaviours are better read as adaptations to insecurity.
**Breakdown:**
- Structural critique: poverty results from interaction of structure and individual traits (✅ Wikipedia, citing Goode & Eames 1996).
- Backlash shifted sociology toward structures and away from "blaming-the-victim" (✅ Wikipedia).
- Newer work avoids a strict structure-versus-culture split (✅ Wikipedia).
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** Carol Stack (1974) called the thesis "fatalistic" (✅ Wikipedia).
**Links:** SOC-2005, SOC-2007, SOC-2016.
**Trap:** Dismissing all evidence of coping behaviour; the critique does not deny lived patterns.
**Mains hook:** Use the causal-direction test: does behaviour cause poverty, or does poverty shape behaviour?
**Back:** *"Adaptation is not a cause."*

**Definition (learn):** Diversity is the presence of distinct linguistic, cultural, ecological or historical identities; regional disparity is unequal development outcomes between regions. A region can be culturally distinct without being disparate, and the reverse.
**Breakdown:**
- Diversity: descriptive, cultural (SOC-2108).
- Disparity: outcome-based, measured by income, infrastructure, human development, fiscal capacity (SOC-2109).
- Regionalism: political assertion (SOC-2110), which can be driven by either axis.
**Key facts / Dates / Data:** (✅ owner 14 basic §1 and advanced §1.)
**Links:** SOC-2108, SOC-2109, SOC-2113, SOC-2119.
**Trap:** Treating cultural difference as evidence of underdevelopment.
**Mains hook:** Define separately before comparing; the 2024 PYQ asks for it in that order.
**Back:** *"Diversity describes; disparity measures."*
